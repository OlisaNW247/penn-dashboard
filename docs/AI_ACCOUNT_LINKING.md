# Students Can't Lend Smooth Their AI Subscriptions

As of 2026-10-21 there is no sanctioned way for Smooth to let a student connect a personal Claude account, and the ChatGPT equivalent exists only behind an OpenAI partner gate that, by its own wording, excludes a hosted, closed-source iOS app today. Anthropic's Claude Code legal page forbids exactly the product shape the owner wants: third-party developers may not **"offer Claude.ai login into their own applications"**, may not **"route requests through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials on behalf of their users"**, and may not even **"collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session tokens"** ([Claude Code: Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance), fetched directly). OpenAI shipped "Sign in with ChatGPT" plan usage at DevDay on **2026-09-29**, but the self-serve path is reserved for "open-source and locally hosted apps" using a `127.0.0.1` loopback callback, only Plus and Pro users can lend their plan, and "paid or remotely hosted apps" are told to fill in an interest form ([OpenAI SIWC token sharing](https://developers.openai.com/siwc/token-sharing-open-source), read via a GitHub mirror of the official Markdown). Apple's App Review Guidelines never mention API keys; in practice free clients that keep a pasted key on-device ship, while a *paid* key-gated app was rejected under 3.1.1 in 2024. The one vendor-provided path that needs no account, no key and no payment, and whose context window fits Smooth's roughly 18,000-token ask prompt, is Apple's **iOS 27 Private Cloud Compute**: a 32K-context reasoning model free of API cost to App Store Small Business Program developers under 2 million downloads, rationed by a per-user daily cap ([Apple: Private Cloud Compute](https://developer.apple.com/private-cloud-compute/), fetched directly). The ranked recommendation is therefore: keep developer-pays as the default and make it cheaper; add PCC as the iOS 27 upgrade of the on-device fallback; file OpenAI's interest form now at zero cost; and offer OpenRouter one-tap sign-in or paste-a-key only as an opt-in for the few students who already hold a funded developer account. A "connect your Claude account" button cannot be built, and a reviewer-visible attempt would breach Anthropic's terms whether or not it worked.

## Anthropic forbids the feature in writing and has enforced it three ways

The authoritative text is short and names every variant Smooth might try. Under "Authentication and credential use", Anthropic states that "OAuth authentication is intended exclusively for purchasers of Claude Free, Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise subscription plans and is designed to support ordinary use of Claude Code and other native Anthropic applications", that developers "should use API key authentication through Claude Console", and that "Anthropic reserves the right to take measures to enforce these restrictions and may do so without prior notice" ([Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)). The Consumer Terms (effective 2025-10-08) add that a user "may not share your Account login information, Anthropic API key, or Account credentials with anyone else" and may not access the service "through automated or non-human means" outside an API key ([Consumer Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)). Both pages were fetched directly on 2026-10-21. The only "Sign in with Claude.ai" string anywhere on Anthropic's properties is the link text in its own magic-link login email ([Log in to your Claude account](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13189465-log-in-to-your-claude-account)).

The 2026 enforcement record shows this is policed, not merely written. Secondary press reports server-side checks on **2026-01-09** that blocked OpenCode, Cline and Roo Code from authenticating against Pro and Max subscriptions, with OpenCode said to have been spoofing Claude Code's client identity through headers ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/anthropic-claudecode-opencode-split/), search summary only). The legal page was updated on **2026-02-19** to say so explicitly ([The Register, 2026-02-20](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/02/20/anthropic-clarifies-ban-on-third-party-tool-access-to-claude/5014546), search summary). The primary GitHub record is the clearest: OpenCode's maintainer wrote on 2026-03-26 that "anthropic has rquested that we do not engage w/ these and that we drop support for oauth entirely" and "We are required to do so for legal reasons" ([anomalyco/opencode#17910](https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode/issues/17910), fetched). Then on 2026-04-03 Boris Cherny posted that from the next day "Claude subscriptions will no longer cover usage on third-party tools like OpenClaw", with extra-usage bundles or an API key as the alternatives, because "our subscriptions weren't built for the usage patterns of these third-party tools" ([bcherny on X](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2040206440556826908), text via search results). A planned May 2026 "Agent SDK credit" that would have given Pro users $20 a month of programmatic credit was paused before it started; the support article of 2026-06-15 says "nothing has changed" and third-party app usage "still draw[s] from your subscription limits" for a user's *own* SDK use ([Use the Claude Agent SDK with your Claude plan](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15036540-use-the-claude-agent-sdk-with-your-claude-plan), fetched). None of that history opens a door for a developer-operated app: the tolerated case is a user running a harness on their own machine with their own token, and even that is now billed as extra usage.

What Anthropic does offer is narrower than the owner's ask and splits into four shapes. First, the developer's own key, which is what Smooth does via OpenRouter today and is the route the legal page directs developers to. Second, the student's own Console key, billed to the student, which since **2026-10-07** can be funded by the **$100 (Max 5x) or $200 (Max 20x) of monthly API credits** that Max and Team plans now include; Pro is excluded, new subscribers wait seven days, and credits must be claimed in a web browser even for plans bought through the iOS app ([API credits for subscribers](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/api-credits-for-subscribers), fetched). Third, the reverse direction: Smooth as a remote MCP connector that the student adds inside Claude. Custom connectors work on Free (one connector), Pro and Max, are added on the web under Customize → Connectors and then used in the Claude iOS app, and can be distributed with a prefilled install link of the form `https://claude.ai/customize/connectors?modal=add-custom-connector&connectorName=NAME&connectorUrl=URL` ([Get started with custom connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11175166-get-started-with-custom-connectors-using-remote-mcp); [Directory vs custom](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/directory-vs-custom), both fetched). A directory listing is open to anyone on a paid plan with "no partner program to apply to first" ([Publish to the directory](https://claude.com/docs/directory/publish), fetched). Fourth, App Attest: an iOS 27 beta path in which the app calls the Claude API directly from the device with "usage billed to your workspace", shipping "no API key" and needing "no proxy", delivered through the `ClaudeForFoundationModels` Swift package ([Anthropic App Attest](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/app-attest), fetched). That last one is developer-pays with the backend removed, not account linking.

The cost picture explains why Anthropic is comfortable pointing developers at their own keys. On the pricing page fetched 2026-10-21, **Claude Haiku 5.5 is $0.10 input / $0.50 output per million tokens** for prompts under 100K, with cache reads at $0.01; Sonnet 5.5 is $2 / $10; Opus 5.5 is $4 / $20 ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). With Smooth's roughly 15,000-token prompt and a 400-token answer, one question on Haiku 5.5 costs about $0.0017 uncached and 300 questions a month about $0.51, or roughly $0.10 with the byte-stable cached prefix the codebase already maintains. A $20 Pro subscription buys the student nothing Smooth can use; a Max student's monthly API credit would cover any plausible dashboard usage many times over, but only if they create and paste a Console key.

## OpenAI sanctions plan sharing, but gates it to partners and desktop loopbacks

OpenAI is the one vendor with a product that matches the owner's wish, and the fine print is what matters. Every OpenAI page in this section was unreachable from the research container and was read either through two GitHub mirrors of the official Markdown or from search snippets, so wording should be re-checked on the live page before anything is built on it. "Sign in with ChatGPT" is two separate capabilities. Identity sign-in is an OpenID Connect login on `auth.openai.com` that hands the app a stable id, name, email and picture, and it "is currently available to selected commercial partners through a limited trial" with a waitlist reached through "your OpenAI representative" ([SIWC quickstart](https://developers.openai.com/siwc/quickstart); [SIWC website guide](https://developers.openai.com/siwc/website), via mirror). ChatGPT plan usage is "an optional capability within Sign in with ChatGPT" under which "Eligible ChatGPT Plus and Pro users can use their plan for AI requests in participating apps", through separate Responses API scopes (`offline_access resource.invoke chatgpt.tokens.use.direct`) and against `https://api.openai.com/v1/responses` with the user's bearer token ([token sharing](https://developers.openai.com/siwc/token-sharing-open-source); [models and inference](https://developers.openai.com/siwc/token-sharing-open-source/models-and-inference), via mirror). The consumer help article confirms the tiering: "Only Plus and Pro users can choose to apply their ChatGPT plan to AI requests", "All users can connect open-source tools. Commercial tools require Plus or Pro", the user sets a per-app weekly cap as a percentage of their plan, and "If plan use fails, the app won't automatically switch to another paid option" ([Using your ChatGPT plan in other apps and sites](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001542-using-your-chatgpt-plan-in-other-apps-and-sites), via mirror). Launch coverage counted 16 partner tools, nearly all developer tooling (Devin, Warp, Notion, Vercel, OpenCode, OpenClaw) ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/sign-in-with-chatgpt/), snippet).

Three documented constraints keep Smooth out as the docs stand. The self-serve registration (`client_id=dynamic_agent_client` with an `ext_agent_host_id`) is described as covering "open-source and locally hosted apps", and the same page says "paid or remotely hosted apps" should "complete the interest form" ([token sharing](https://developers.openai.com/siwc/token-sharing-open-source), via mirror); Smooth's `ask` runs on a Supabase Edge Function, which is remotely hosted by any reading. The only documented redirect is "an HTTP loopback callback on `127.0.0.1`, for example `http://127.0.0.1:1455/auth/callback`", with "Only the port may vary" and an instruction not to substitute `localhost` ([SIWC sign-in flow](https://developers.openai.com/siwc/token-sharing-open-source/sign-in), via mirror); no custom scheme or universal link is mentioned, and `ASWebAuthenticationSession` cannot intercept an `http://` callback, so iOS is unsupported until OpenAI documents a native flow. The preview limitations would also force a rewrite of Smooth's prompt: requests must set `store: false` and `stream: true`, explicit system-role items are rejected, and `max_output_tokens`, `temperature`, `prompt_cache_retention` and `user` are among the parameters that must be omitted ([preview limitations](https://developers.openai.com/siwc/token-sharing-open-source/preview-limitations), via mirror). The required UI is also prescriptive: a "Continue with ChatGPT" button, a "You're using your ChatGPT plan" first-time modal, a "Manage usage" link to `chatgpt.com/settings/usage`, and a pricing page that says which of the app's plans support plan usage ([UI/UX guidelines](https://developers.openai.com/siwc/ui-ux-guidelines), via mirror). Students on Free or Go, the common tiers, get identity only even where it works.

Reusing the Codex CLI's login is the tempting shortcut and is the wrong one. Codex signs in with a PKCE flow against `auth.openai.com` using OpenAI's own public client id `app_EMoamEEZ73f0CkXaXp7hrann` and a loopback on port 1455, and the resulting token works only on ChatGPT's private `chatgpt.com/backend-api/codex/responses`, not on `api.openai.com` ([community gist describing auth.json](https://gist.github.com/ravidsrk/4e72b774c044917cd260560ec5831e1d); [Codex auth docs](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/auth), via mirror). OpenAI's own plan-usage page now says "Don't use ChatGPT's `backend-api` endpoints for this flow" ([models and inference](https://developers.openai.com/siwc/token-sharing-open-source/models-and-inference), via mirror), and the community plugins that do it carry disclaimers such as "Not for: Commercial services, API resale, or multi-user applications" ([open-hax/codex](https://github.com/open-hax/codex)). No ban wave, takedown or explicit prohibition was found, which is absence of evidence only; a hosted consumer app impersonating OpenAI's client id against a private endpoint is not a foundation to ship on. OpenAI's API-key rules are equally unhelpful for a paste-a-key design routed through Smooth's server: the Business Terms forbid users to "buy, sell, or transfer API keys from, to or with a third party", and the key-safety article says "The sharing of API keys is against the Terms of Use" and that requests "should go through your own backend" ([Best practices for API key safety](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/5112595-best-practices-for-api-key-safety), snippet). A key held in the phone's Keychain and sent only to `api.openai.com` is unaddressed rather than permitted, which is the status every BYOK client lives with.

The reverse direction exists at OpenAI too and is a cleaner fit on paper than at Anthropic, with two guideline snags. The Apps SDK became the "plugin" format on **2026-07-09** (skills plus an MCP server plus optional UI, one directory shared by ChatGPT and Codex, "visible across ChatGPT plans"), submissions need a verified individual or business identity, an OAuth 2.1 server with protected-resource metadata, a privacy policy, suitability for ages 13 and up, and a demo login for reviewers ([Plugins in Codex](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001256-plugins-in-codex), snippet; [plugin submission](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/deploy/submission) and [plugin auth](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/build/auth), via mirror). The model inference inside ChatGPT is ChatGPT's, so the student's plan pays and Smooth hosts only an MCP server. But the guidelines say "We cannot approve plugins that primarily function as unofficial connectors to third-party services" and "Selling digital products or services—including subscriptions, digital content, tokens, or credits—is not allowed" ([plugin guidelines](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/plugin-guidelines), via mirror); Smooth is in substance an unofficial Canvas, Gradescope and Ed connector, and a PennKey cannot be handed to a reviewer, so a server-side demo account would have to exist. Custom GPTs with OAuth Actions, the older route, are being retired: creation of new GPTs was planned to end 2026-09-25 and all custom GPTs "stop running" on **2026-12-11**, with "custom actions" not migrating ([Custom GPT retirement FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001519-custom-gpt-retirement-and-migration-faq), via mirror and snippet). Do not build on Actions.

## Apple tolerates on-device keys, and iOS 27 Private Cloud Compute is the no-account path

The live App Review Guidelines, last revised **2026-10-16**, do not contain the words "API key" ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=rzwoyszz), both fetched). What they do say shapes every option below. Guideline 3.1.1 requires in-app purchase "to unlock features or functionality" and names "license keys" as a forbidden unlock mechanism. The 3.1.3 exceptions are closed lists that do not include AI: "Reader" apps cover "magazines, newspapers, books, audio, music, and video"; "Free Stand-alone Apps" cover "VoIP, Cloud Storage, Email Services, or Web Hosting"; and Multiplatform Services speak of items "acquired in your app on other platforms or your web site", i.e. the developer's own service. A free app that sells nothing and merely connects to an account the student already has therefore argues that 3.1.1 is not triggered at all, rather than that an exception applies, and that argument has no published Apple ruling behind it. The one documented data point cuts both ways: in September 2024 a *paid* "insert your OpenAI/Claude key" app was rejected with the words "the app uses API keys to unlock or enable functionality" ([Apple Developer Forums thread 763884](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/763884)), while free BYOK clients such as Pal Chat (App Store id 6447545085, free with an optional Pro IAP, keys and history on-device, Claude 4.6 named in the listing) have been live for years ([Pal Chat listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pal-chat-ai-chat-client/id6447545085), snippet). The pattern that survives review is a key that unlocks nothing the developer sells, stored on the device, with the app fully usable without it, which Smooth's developer-paid path and on-device fallback already guarantee.

Three other rules bear on account linking. Guideline 4.8 (Login Services) requires an equivalent private login only when a third-party login is used "to set up or authenticate the user's primary account with the app"; a Claude, ChatGPT or OpenRouter link attached to one feature while the anonymous Supabase account stays primary is arguably outside it, and the exemptions for "an education ... app that requires the user to sign in with an existing education ... account" and "a client for a specific third-party service" plausibly cover Smooth, though a reviewer could still ask for Sign in with Apple, which would give a student no AI credit. Guideline 5.1.1(v) says an app "may not store credentials or tokens to social networks off of the device and may only use such credentials or tokens to directly connect to the social network from the app itself while the app is in use"; written for social networks, it is the safe reading for any AI token, and it means a student's key must never transit Smooth's Edge Function. The 2026 text of 5.1.2(i) adds that "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so", which applies to any option that sends the context document somewhere new, and the new 2.5.19 on "hidden, dormant, or undocumented features" means a BYOK field cannot be tucked behind a debug flag the reviewer cannot find ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Linking out to a third party's billing page is a lower bar than it was: since the April 30, 2025 contempt order and Apple's May 1, 2025 revision, "entitlements are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps", the Ninth Circuit upheld the contempt on **2025-12-11** while allowing a cost-based commission on the developer's *own* linked-out sales, and the Supreme Court granted certiorari on **2026-06-30** with no merits decision yet ([Ninth Circuit opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2025/12/11/25-2935.pdf); [Apple's merits brief](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-1311/424150/20260914154510009_2026-09-14%20No.%2025-1311%20Apple-Epic%20Merits%20Opening%20Brief.pdf)). A link from Smooth to openrouter.ai or claude.ai to buy credits from someone else is not even the developer's digital good; outside the US storefront it is still a forbidden call to action, so an informational sentence with no button is the safe global copy.

Apple's own models are the one route with no account, no key and no payment, and the iOS 26 version is too small for Smooth's question shape. The on-device model is "a large language model with 3 billion parameters, each quantized to 2 bits", which Apple says "is not designed for world knowledge or advanced reasoning" ([WWDC25 session 286](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/), fetched). Its context is **4,096 tokens per session, shared** by instructions, prompts, tool schemas and the response; overflow throws `.exceededContextWindowSize` and Apple's recommended pattern for documents is retrieval of snippets ([TN3193](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3193-managing-the-on-device-foundation-model-s-context-window), fetched). Smooth's current excerpt budget alone (eight passages of 1,500 characters, about 3,000 to 4,000 tokens) fills that window before the question, which is why the existing `OnDeviceLanguageModel` is a rephrasing step and not an answerer. iOS 27, announced 2026-06-08, keeps the on-device window at 4K ("the context size is the same as it was before. It's 4096 and it is a shared window") but adds `PrivateCloudComputeLanguageModel`: the same server model behind Apple Intelligence, with **a 32,000-token context window and reasoning**, where "you don't have to worry about account setup, you don't have to deal with authentication, and you don't have to store API keys" and "No prompts are ever stored" ([WWDC26 session 241](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/); [WWDC26 Q&A session 8121](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/8121/); [Adding server-side intelligence with PCC](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/FoundationModels/adding-server-side-intelligence-with-private-cloud-compute), all fetched). Smooth's 14.5K-token context document plus excerpts fits inside 32K with room for the answer.

PCC's price is zero for Smooth's size, with an application and a cap. Apple's eligibility page says developers "in the App Store Small Business Program with fewer than 2 million first-time App Store downloads will be able to use Apple Foundation Models running on Private Cloud Compute (PCC) with no cloud API cost", provided the "Private Cloud Compute entitlement" is assigned to the account; crossing the threshold triggers a six-month migration notice ([Apple: Private Cloud Compute](https://developer.apple.com/private-cloud-compute/), fetched). Each user "gets a daily request limit" shared with Apple's own server features, the framework throws `quotaLimitReached` and exposes a `resetDate` and an iCloud+ upsell, and Apple warns the service "may hit rate limiting ... when the system is busy" ([PCC documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/FoundationModels/adding-server-side-intelligence-with-private-cloud-compute); [session 8121](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/8121/)). The numeric limit is unpublished. Eligibility on the student's side is iOS 27 on an Apple Intelligence device (iPhone 15 Pro or later, every iPhone 16 and 17), which one analyst estimated excludes about 65% of the global installed base in October 2025 ([Wccftech citing CLSA](https://wccftech.com/the-case-for-apple-iphones-upcoming-super-upgrade-cycle-based-on-just-2-simple-facts/amp/), snippet); no Penn-specific figure exists, and the honest way to learn it is to log `SystemLanguageModel.availability` reasons on-device. Two adjacent Apple developments do not help: the reported iOS 27 "Extensions" feature is the reverse direction, an AI vendor's app supplying Siri and Writing Tools, not an API an unrelated app can call ([9to5Mac, 2026-05-05](https://9to5mac.com/2026/05/05/ios-27-will-let-you-choose-between-gemini-claude-and-more-for-ai-features-report/), snippet; it was not presented at WWDC), and the Anthropic and Google Swift packages Apple said "will soon extend the Foundation Models framework" are told by Apple to offer "a token provider or sign in flow" rather than a key string ([WWDC26 session 339](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/339/), fetched), which under Anthropic's terms can only mean a developer credential, as App Attest already shows.

## Comparable apps resell or paste; none has removed the payment step

The survey of roughly forty apps, assembled entirely from search snippets of their listings and docs, splits cleanly by audience. Every consumer and student product checked (Notion AI, Perplexity, Poe, Dia, Bolt, Superhuman, Granola, Quizlet, Chegg, Knowt, StudyFetch, Mindgrasp, Turbolearn) resells inference under its own subscription and offers no BYOK at all; paste-your-key lives in developer tools and chat clients whose users already own a console account (Cursor, Zed, Warp, Raycast, Obsidian plugins, TypingMind, Chatbox, Msty, Jan, GitHub Copilot) ([Cursor API keys](https://cursor.com/help/models-and-usage/api-keys.md); [Zed custom API keys](https://zed.dev/docs/ai/custom-api-keys); [Warp BYOK](https://docs.warp.dev/support-and-billing/plans-and-pricing/bring-your-own-api-key)). Raycast is the instructive case: it launched BYOK on macOS on 2025-06-11 as "send as many AI messages as you want at your own cost without a Pro subscription", shipped it on iOS on **2026-05-29**, and then in its v2 note made BYOK require a Pro subscription because "all of these options use the same Raycast product around the model" ([Raycast v1.100.0](https://www.raycast.com/changelog/1-100-0); [iOS 1.2.13](https://www.raycast.com/changelog/ios/1-2-13); [Changes to Bring Your Own AI in v2](https://manual.raycast.com/ai/byoai.md)). Raycast also routes Anthropic, OpenAI and Google BYOK calls "through our servers" while sending OpenRouter calls direct, which drew a user complaint about undisclosed server routing ([Raycast BYOK manual](https://manual.raycast.com/ai/bring-your-own-key)). On iOS specifically, at least six small BYOK clients (Pal Chat, Chatbox, Geeps, Open Chat, LLM Chat Client, Oriveo) plus Raycast accept pasted Anthropic or OpenAI keys and are live on the App Store, with copy that stresses "stored securely in your device's keychain" and "get an API key first" ([Open Chat listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/open-chat-inference/id6758454746); [Geeps listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/geeps-ai-chat-byok/id6476961115), snippets). No app in the survey publishes conversion or drop-off figures for its key-entry funnel, so the owner's belief that pasting is "too clunky for most students" is unrefuted by data but also unsupported by any.

The one widely deployed "sign in, no paste" mechanism for cloud models is OpenRouter's PKCE flow, and it moves the paste but not the payment. The app opens `https://openrouter.ai/auth` with a `callback_url`, `code_challenge` and `code_challenge_method=S256`, receives a one-time `code`, and POSTs it with the verifier to `https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/auth/keys` to receive a long-lived user-owned key, with no client registration, no secret and no refresh token ([OpenRouter OAuth PKCE](https://openrouter.ai/docs/use-cases/oauth-pkce), official page cited via snippet and a community implementer's notes in [Starbugstone/openRouter_UI#6](https://github.com/Starbugstone/openRouter_UI/issues/6)). The documented callback shapes are `https` URLs and `localhost`; a developer's dated code comment records that a custom scheme "does not work. Verified 2026-08-02: openrouter.ai/auth accepts whispershortcut://... unauthenticated, but once the user is signed in it silently drops the request and lands them on the homepage" ([mgsgde/whisper-shortcut](https://github.com/mgsgde/whisper-shortcut)). On iOS the compatible stack is `ASWebAuthenticationSession` with an `.https(host:path:)` universal-link callback (iOS 17.4 and later) on a domain Smooth controls with an `apple-app-site-association` file ([ASWebAuthenticationSession.Callback](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/authenticationservices/aswebauthenticationsession/callback), fetched), which also fixes the fact that the consent screen and key are named after the callback origin. One native client, RouterChat (App Store id 6776368852, first released 2026-06-12), ships "sign in with OpenRouter or use an API key" with keys in the Keychain ([RouterChat via mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/routerchat/6776368852), snippet). The limits matter for students: the OAuth key cannot read the account balance (`/api/v1/credits` needs a management key, so the app sees only the key's own `limit_remaining`); a zero or negative balance returns **402 even on `:free` models**; accounts that have bought fewer than 10 credits get 50 free-model requests a day; and credit purchases carry a **5.5% fee with a $0.80 minimum**, so the smallest useful top-up is about $5.80 ([OpenRouter limits](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api-reference/limits), snippet; [TrueFoundry on OpenRouter pricing](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/openrouter-pricing), community). "Sign in with OpenRouter" for a student is therefore "sign up, pay at least $5.80, then sign in", which is lower friction than a Console key but not the one-tap connection the owner pictured.

The remaining friction patterns are cheap and each removes one step. No vendor key page accepts a parameter to pre-name a key or return it to an app: Anthropic shows an `sk-ant-` key "only once, at creation" and requires prepaid credits beyond a small starter grant ([Get your Claude API key](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/get-api-key), fetched); OpenAI keys are likewise shown once and effectively need a funded org; Google AI Studio is the only one that issues a working key in one sitting without a card, at the price that the free tier's "content may be used to improve Google products", which for Smooth would mean course material and the student's work list ([Gemini API billing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/billing), snippet). On the phone, `UIPasteControl` (iOS 16 and later) lets the student paste "without that prompt" that programmatic pasteboard reads raise, and `detectedPatterns` can decide whether to show the button "without notifying the user" ([UIPasteControl](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uipastecontrol); [UIPasteboard](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uipasteboard), fetched); Universal Clipboard carries a key copied on a Mac to the phone's pasteboard within Bluetooth range ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/imac/apd372a5d2a6/2020/mac/11), snippet); `DataScannerViewController` reads a QR code, but only helps if Smooth hosts the page that renders it, since no vendor key page does ([DataScannerViewController](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/visionkit/datascannerviewcontroller), fetched). An RFC 8628-style "open smooth.example/link on your laptop, enter this code, paste the key there" page on Supabase is a day of work and avoids both the shown-once problem and the paste prompt, but it moves only identity, never payment, and the key then transits Smooth's server unless it is encrypted on the web page to a per-device public key the phone generated. Server-side custody of other people's keys is exactly what the **OpenCode Console breach of 2026-10-17/19** punished: 1.4% of Console users had their OpenAI, Anthropic and Google keys exposed, one reporting "more than $1,700 in unauthorized charges overnight", while the open-source app and CLI were unaffected because "those keys never come to us" ([TechCrunch, 2026-10-19](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/19/hackers-hit-ai-coding-tool-opencode-running-up-charges-on-users-ai-accounts/), snippet). Phone-only storage is the privacy story and the liability shield at once.

Developer-pays, which Smooth already does, is cheap enough that the whole account-linking question is as much about cost as about principle. At list prices read on 2026-10-21, a 14,000-input / 500-output question costs about **$0.0064 on gpt-4.1-mini uncached and $0.0025 cached**, $0.0045 / $0.0016 on gpt-5-mini, and $0.00165 / $0.00048 on Claude Haiku 5.5; a heavy student asking 20 questions a day for a month costs $3.84 / $1.50 on gpt-4.1-mini, $2.70 / $0.96 on gpt-5-mini, and $0.99 / $0.29 on Haiku 5.5 ([LiteLLM price table](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BerriAI/litellm/main/model_prices_and_context_window.json), community-maintained, fetched; [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), fetched). The OpenAI figures are from a community table because the official pricing page was unreachable; verify before quoting. If any of that were ever charged back through IAP, Apple's Small Business Program takes 15% rather than 30% ([Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/), fetched), and selling "AI credits" makes them consumables squarely under 3.1.1, with a web-bought alternative allowed under 3.1.3(b) only if the same item is also sold in-app.

The first two rows are the real answer. Developer-pays already gives every Penn student an account-free ask, costs well under a dollar per active student-month on a cached cheap model, and is the only shape Anthropic names as correct for "developers building products or services that interact with Claude's capabilities" ([Legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)). Private Cloud Compute is its natural complement: it serves the students the backend does not (non-Penn installs run fully on-device today), it keeps Apple out of the prompt path's storage ("No prompts are ever stored"), it needs no key in the binary and no proxy to run, and it fits the prompt Smooth already builds. Its costs are organisational before they are technical: the publishing team must be in the Small Business Program and hold the entitlement, the per-user daily cap is unpublished and shared with Siri, and the eligible population is iPhone 15 Pro or newer on iOS 27. The right first step is a one-line on-device log of `SystemLanguageModel.availability` reasons, which never leaves the phone, to learn what share of the Penn install base PCC would reach. Before either path sends the context document to any new recipient, the known gap in `docs/PRIVACY.md` (the ask summary carries completion state while the policy says it does not) should be closed, because 5.1.2(i)'s "third-party AI" disclosure will be read against the policy as written.

Options three through five are what remains of "connect your own account", and each should be sized honestly. OpenAI's plan-sharing is the only vendor mechanism that matches the owner's intent, and filing the interest form costs nothing; but the docs as mirrored exclude "remotely hosted" apps, document no native redirect, and reject the system-role prompt and `max_output_tokens` Smooth's `ask` function uses, so admission would mean a phone-direct Responses API path with a reworked prompt and OpenAI's prescribed buttons, for Plus and Pro students only. OpenRouter sign-in is buildable today on iOS and is the one way to remove the paste step without any vendor's blessing being needed, since OpenRouter's PKCE flow needs no registration; but a new account has no balance, free models 402 at zero, and the student cannot even be shown their remaining balance from the key the app holds, so the experience is "pay OpenRouter $5.80 first". Because the key must stay on the phone (5.1.1(v)'s spirit, the OpenCode breach, and for Claude keys Anthropic's ban on intermediation), the phone has to assemble the same prompt the Edge Function does, which duplicates `excerptBlock`, `RecencyIntent` and the context document's cache discipline in Swift, and the backend's quota and `ask_outcomes` telemetry stop seeing those questions. Paste-a-key adds the three first-party vendors to the same screen for a week's work and is the only way a Claude model ever answers under a student's own money; the Max plan's $100 to $200 monthly API credit makes it free for the rare Max student, and Pro students get nothing. All three change the privacy policy in the same direction: a new named recipient of the question, the excerpts and the context document, with an explicit in-app permission step, while the sentence that credentials never leave the phone stays true only if nothing is ever relayed through Supabase.

Three things cannot be done, and the report should be read as closing them rather than leaving them open. A Claude account link, in any form (OAuth, a stored session token, a relayed `claude setup-token`, a hidden WebView into claude.ai), is forbidden by text Anthropic has enforced by server-side detection, by a legal request to a 157,000-star open-source project, and by billing; Smooth's Canvas-style hidden-WebView pattern must not be pointed at claude.ai. Reusing the Codex CLI's client id and private `backend-api` endpoint is undocumented, told against by OpenAI's own plan-usage page, and would make a hosted consumer app the exact "multi-user application" the community plugins disclaim. Apple's ChatGPT integration in Siri and Writing Tools exposes no API, and the reported iOS 27 Extensions feature runs the other way. Reselling Claude usage to students is barred by Anthropic ("may not pay for, resell, or intermediate Claude usage on their end users' behalf") and would be an in-app digital good under 3.1.1 besides, so the two rulebooks agree: either the developer pays and sells nothing, or the student brings an account whose vendor allows it.

## Conclusion

The question "can students connect their own Claude or ChatGPT account" has turned out to be a question about who is allowed to be in the request path, and the two vendors have answered it in opposite directions for the same reason: capacity. Anthropic treats a consumer subscription as spendable only inside its own applications and has written the prohibition broadly enough that even holding a student's token is a breach; OpenAI treats the subscription as a spendable allowance but rations it with per-app caps, Plus/Pro gating and a partner list that so far contains developer tools. A free student dashboard with a Supabase backend sits outside both programmes as they stand, and the shortcut that looks available (reusing Codex's login) is the one that would end badly.

What has changed in the last year is the arrival of a third party that does not need an account at all. Apple's Private Cloud Compute inverts the economics the owner was trying to escape: instead of the student lending Smooth a subscription, Apple lends Smooth a model, free below 2 million downloads, with the student's identity never leaving the device. Paired with a cheaper cached model on the developer-paid path, it makes the per-student cost of ask small enough that account linking stops being a cost problem and becomes purely a preference, and preferences can wait for OpenAI's partner door to open. The practical sequence is to measure iOS 27 reach on the Penn install base, file the OpenAI form while it costs nothing, and treat key paste and OpenRouter sign-in as power-user doors that exist for the student who already has a funded console, not as the way most students will ever reach the tree.
