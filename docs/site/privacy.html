Low Hanging Fruit — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-10-27

Low Hanging Fruit ("LHF", "the app") is a personal academic dashboard that shows your Canvas assignments and deadlines in one place. This policy explains what the app does with your information.

In short: your grades and your Canvas/Gradescope logins stay on your phone. Your list of work stays there too, with three exceptions described below: a summary of it, including which items are done, goes with each question you ask "the tree"; if you pair the Smooth Chrome extension, it travels end-to-end encrypted between your own phone and your own laptop, where we cannot read it; and if you turn on iCloud sync, it goes to your own iCloud. Course materials — syllabi, course pages, assignment descriptions, announcements — are pooled with classmates in the same Canvas course, so everyone shares one copy instead of each of you re-fetching it. Questions you ask "the tree" are answered by an AI model running on our server. The question and the answer are not stored; a small record of each request (when, which model, how long, how many tokens, never the text) is. Deleting the app does not remove everything it saved on the phone: see "Deleting your data".

What stays on your phone

  • Your Canvas and Gradescope logins. You sign in through each service's own web page inside the app. Session cookies and your Canvas calendar feed URL are stored in this device's Keychain. For Penn Canvas, the sign-in screen explains that Smooth also saves the PennKey credentials you submit after a successful connection, to reconnect automatically. Gradescope and other schools' passwords are not captured. See "Automatic Canvas reconnection" below. iOS keeps Keychain items when an app is deleted, so these can outlast the app; see "Deleting your data" below.
  • Your grades, completions, and submission state. What you've turned in, what you've checked off, any grade the Grade Watcher has observed — all of it is kept on this phone. Grades and scores are never sent to our server. Three things carry part of it off the phone, and only these: whether each item is done (never a grade) goes to our server in the summary sent with each question to "the tree" (see "What is sent to our server"); completions go, end-to-end encrypted, to a laptop you pair (see "The Chrome extension"); and if you turn on iCloud sync, your record of each item, including the score you earned on it, goes to your own iCloud (see "iCloud sync").
  • Your work list and your name. Assignments you add yourself, due-date edits, reminder settings, and the first name you enter never go to our server. The work Canvas and Gradescope give you is the exception: its titles and due dates are in the summary sent with each question you ask "the tree" (see "What is sent to our server"), though your own tasks and your due-date edits are not. Your own tasks and due-date edits are shared, encrypted, with a laptop you pair (below). With iCloud sync on, your own tasks and your per-class settings go to your own iCloud (below). Your name is never part of either sync, and your reminder settings are never part of the laptop's.
  • The widget snapshot. A small "next due" summary written to a private container shared only between the app and its widget on your device.
  • The announcements list. The megaphone on the dashboard lists your classes' Canvas announcements from the last 60 days. For that the app keeps each one's title, class, date, link and a short preview in a file on this phone. That file is never uploaded, and disconnecting Canvas deletes it. For a class that uses Ed Discussion, the list also shows that class's Ed announcements and pinned posts, and an instructor's or TA's post while it still matters, taken from the course material already on your phone (described below); showing them makes no new request and sends nothing. Which posts stay listed is decided on this phone: a post leaves once what it was about is over, or once every task found in it is done. For that the app keeps, per post, a short note of what kind of post it is and until when it matters (see "AI assist" below). The note stays on this phone, is never uploaded, and disconnecting Canvas deletes it. Whether you finished a task is never sent anywhere to make this decision.
  • Tasks found in announcements. When an announcement says to do something by a date (and, for a class that uses Ed Discussion, when an Ed announcement or pinned post does), the app turns it into a card on your list. The card and a task you check off stay on this phone with the rest of your work list, and a task leaves your list once its date has passed. The only thing that can leave the phone is the post's text, and only if AI assist is on (see "What is sent to our server").
  • Due dates you change. If you edit an assignment's due date, the new date is saved on this phone and used for your list, your reminders and the widget. It is never uploaded in readable form (only inside the encrypted sync with your own paired laptop): a question you ask "the tree" is sent with the date Canvas or Gradescope gave, not the one you set.
  • Your voice. The mic in "the tree" turns what you say into text on this phone, with Apple's on-device speech recognition. The audio never leaves the phone and is not kept; only the text you then choose to send goes anywhere. On a phone or language where on-device recognition is not available, the mic is simply not shown rather than sending audio to a server.

iCloud sync (optional, off by default)

Profile → sync has a switch, "sync between my devices". It is off until you turn it on, and it takes effect the next time you open the app. With it on, the app keeps two things in your own iCloud account, using Apple's CloudKit and iCloud key-value storage, so your other devices signed in to the same Apple ID (a Mac running Smooth, for example) see them:

  • Your record of your work. For each item: its class, title, due date and link, where it came from, whether you checked it off or it was turned in (and when), the score you earned and its maximum when Canvas or Gradescope has given one, and the tasks you added yourself.
  • Your per-class settings. The names you gave classes, which classes are hidden or deleted, each class's reminder choices, and the app's per-class choices about course content.

It does not carry your name, your Canvas or Gradescope logins, cookies, calendar feed address or saved PennKey password (those stay in each device's own Keychain and are never synced), your grade history over time, or due dates you changed.

This copy lives in your iCloud, under your Apple ID, with the protection Apple gives iCloud data. It does not pass through our server, and we cannot read it. Turning the switch off stops new changes going up; it does not delete what is already in your iCloud, and neither does deleting the app. You can remove it in iOS Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage.

Automatic Canvas reconnection

Staying connected is part of signing in to Penn Canvas with Smooth; there is no separate "stay signed in" choice or second password entry.

  • What is captured: the username and password submitted on the verified PennKey login form. Smooth holds one pair in memory while Canvas verifies the session, for at most ten minutes. It does not record keystrokes or Duo codes. Cancelled, failed, or expired attempts release the temporary pair.
  • When it is saved: only after Canvas successfully connects. The login screen explains this before you enter your credentials.
  • Where: the iOS/macOS Keychain, encrypted at rest, this-device-only, never synced to iCloud or other devices. Credentials are never logged, written to preferences, or sent to Smooth's servers.
  • Use: to fill Penn's own HTTPS login page when Canvas requires a new session. Duo approval may still be required. A rejected stored password stops automatic retries and asks you to reconnect or update the password.
  • How to remove it: disconnect Canvas in Settings. This removes the saved credentials and stops automatic reconnection.
  • SSO and other schools: if a login succeeds without submitting a PennKey password, Smooth can retain the session but cannot manufacture a saved password. Other institutions continue using their existing session handling.

What is sent to our server (and why)

LHF now runs a small backend of our own (Supabase: a Postgres database plus a few serverless functions) so that two things work better than they could on-device alone:

  • Course materials, pooled per class. After you connect Canvas, the app fetches your class's syllabus, course pages, module listings, assignment descriptions, and announcements using your own logged-in session — exactly as it always has — and uploads the extracted text to our server, keyed by Canvas's own course id. Every student in the same Canvas course site shares one copy: if a classmate already synced it, you get it instantly instead of waiting on your own fetch, and a new student gets the whole course's material on day one. We record which Canvas section you're in for possible future use, but material isn't split by section yet — see "Anonymous accounts" below for the limits of this design. This sync is automatic: it runs once after you connect Canvas, then again on the app's normal hourly refresh if a course's material is stale. There's no manual sync button and nothing to type in.

    Ed Discussion, for classes that use it (Penn, since 2026-10-02). If a class's Canvas site has an "Ed Discussion" tool, the app opens it the way you would, signed in through your own Canvas session, and reads the class's Ed board. It keeps only the posts that are course material: announcements, pinned posts, and posts written by the instructor or TAs. Student posts and questions, private posts, and anonymous posts never leave your phone, and no author's name is ever recorded. The kept posts are pooled per class exactly like a syllabus. You never type an Ed password or token; the Ed session the app receives is stored in this phone's Keychain like your Canvas session and deleted with it. (The "AI assist" switch below also covers these posts.)

    What is never included in that upload: your grades, completions, submission state, your work list, your name, or anything else specific to you. Only the shared, public-to-the-class content described above. The only other request the app makes on its own is the anonymous update-policy check described below.

  • Questions you ask "the tree." When you ask a question, the app sends the question, a summary of your classes and work that the app builds on your phone, the most relevant excerpts of the pooled course material, the Canvas ids of your classes, and the time of the question and your phone's time zone (so that due times and "tomorrow" read correctly) to our server, which forwards them to an AI model to generate an answer streamed back to you. Neither the question nor the answer is stored anywhere — see "What we keep and for how long" below.

    What the summary holds: for each class, its code, any name you gave it, and the grading weights (percentages) from its syllabus or Grade Watcher; and, for each item of work from Canvas and Gradescope, each reading, and each task the app found in an announcement, its class, its title, the due date Canvas or Gradescope gave, where it came from, and whether it is done (you checked it off or it was turned in), still outstanding, or has nothing to submit. So your completion state is sent: one word per item, beside the item. The summary does not hold your name, any grade or score, a due date you changed, a task you added yourself, or your reminder settings.

    The Announcement Watcher's "AI assist" toggle is on by default (you can turn it off in Settings). With it on, extracting a task and due date from an announcement's text works the same way — the announcement text goes to the same server and model, and the server doesn't store the result either. Each of these requests carries the post's title and body, the post's id (a Canvas announcement's id, or the Ed thread's), the class code and the post's date, and nothing else about you. Since 2026-10-18 the same switch covers Ed Discussion announcements and pinned posts for classes that use it (Penn): such a post's title and body (never an author's name, which the app does not keep) go to the same server and model, with the class code, the post's id and the date, once, only when an on-device check says the text could contain a task, and only for a class you have not hidden. It is text the app already pools per class (see above). Since 2026-10-23 an instructor's or TA's ordinary Ed post is sent the same way, once, a few a day at most, so the model can say whether it still matters; no task is ever made from such a post. The answer to every one of these requests also says what kind of post it is and until when it matters; the phone keeps that short note (and the tasks, as above), and the server keeps nothing. A post read before the server could give that note is sent once more, only for the note, a few a day at most and only while it is under two weeks old; the second answer's tasks are thrown away. With the switch off, Canvas and Ed posts are read on the phone and nothing is sent.

    Grade Watcher can also ask the server how a class's Canvas assignment groups line up with its syllabus categories. That request carries the names of the groups and assignments, their points possible and submission types — never a score, never whether you submitted anything. The answer is a suggestion the report shows you; nothing changes until you tap "use it." Because the structure of a class's assignment groups is the same for everyone in it, the answer is cached per class and shared with classmates, so the model is asked about each class once, not once per student.

  • An anonymous account id. The first time you launch the app, it creates an anonymous account with our backend — no email, no password, no name — so the server can tell your enrollment and quota apart from every other student's without knowing who you are. See "Anonymous accounts" below.

Offline, or if you've asked more questions than the daily limit, or if our server is unreachable, ask still works — it answers from what's already synced to your phone, exactly as it did before this backend existed, just without the AI model's help on questions that need it.

Keeping the app up to date

On launch, and whenever you bring LHF back to the foreground, the app makes a plain request to a small public file the developer hosts (for example on GitHub), to check whether your copy of the app is still supported. That request has no query parameters, no headers identifying you or your device, and carries nothing about your Canvas or Gradescope data, your grades, or your coursework — it is a version check, not a phone-home. Like any request to any website, whoever's server answers it can see an IP address, the way it could for any page you load, but nothing else about you or your coursework. If the check finds your build is too old, LHF can show a full-screen notice that stops the app from opening again until you update; if a newer version simply exists, it shows a dismissible banner instead. If the check fails for any reason — no signal, the file is unreachable, anything — the app opens normally and relies on the last successful check, kept for up to 30 days, in the meantime.

The Chrome extension (optional, Penn)

Smooth has a Chrome extension for your laptop. It is optional and does nothing until you pair it with your phone.

  • It uses the Canvas, Gradescope and Ed Discussion sessions already open in your browser. It never reads, copies or stores a cookie or a password; Chrome attaches your own session to its requests, the same way it does when you open those sites. Your Canvas calendar feed address is looked up fresh each time and never saved. Your Ed session token is kept only in Chrome's memory and is gone when Chrome closes.
  • It collects course material your phone can't reach: every course announcement, every course page, and the text of PDFs in a course's Files (slides, readings), plus Ed announcements, pinned posts and staff posts under the same rule as the phone (never a student's post, never an author's name). That material is pooled per class exactly like the phone's ("What is sent to our server"), so classmates share one copy.
  • Pairing. The extension shows a QR code; you scan it with your iPhone and confirm "pair this laptop?" in Smooth. The code contains the secret key itself, made on the laptop, so anyone who can see or photograph the code while it is on screen could read the key: pair in private. The key goes from the laptop's screen to your phone's camera and is never sent to our server. The code works once, expires after ten minutes, and links nothing unless your own phone confirms; an unused code leaves nothing saved on the laptop. The server only learns that the extension's anonymous account now belongs with your phone's.
  • End-to-end encrypted sync. With that key, your phone and laptop share what you've checked off, due dates you've changed, tasks you've added, and your list of what's due. Each device encrypts its copy (AES-256-GCM) before it leaves, and our server stores only that encrypted copy. We cannot read it, and neither can Supabase. Your name, reminder settings and grades are never part of it.
  • What the laptop keeps. In the extension's storage in Chrome, on your laptop: the secret key, the extension's anonymous sign-in, its own copy of your checked-off items, due-date edits, tasks and list of what's due, and, after each sync, your phone's list of what's due. Chrome stores that the way it stores any extension's data; the extension adds no encryption of its own on the laptop, so protect the laptop (a screen lock and disk encryption) as you would for any saved login.
  • Unpairing (from the extension, or Profile → accounts → laptop on the phone) deletes the extension's account and its encrypted copy from our server, and removes the key and your phone's list from the laptop. The extension keeps its own copy of your checked-off items, due-date edits and tasks there until you remove the extension or clear its data. Disconnecting Canvas on the phone does the same on the server side for every paired laptop, along with everything listed under "Deleting your data".

Who we send data to

  • Supabase hosts our database and server functions. They act as our infrastructure provider; they don't get a separate copy for their own use beyond running the service.
  • OpenRouter, and the AI model providers it routes to, process the questions "the tree" is asked (and, if AI assist is on, announcement and Ed post text) in order to generate an answer. We send every request with OpenRouter's data-collection-deny setting turned on, which instructs providers on that route not to retain or train on the request. We don't control what OpenRouter or upstream providers do beyond honoring that setting, but it's the strictest option they offer.

We do not sell data, run ads, or share anything with data brokers, and we have no relationship with Supabase or OpenRouter beyond paying for infrastructure and API access.

What we keep and for how long

  • Course materials are kept as long as they're current, shared per Canvas course as described above. They're not tied to you individually — deleting your account doesn't delete a course's shared material, because it isn't your data; it's the course site's public content.
  • Questions and answers are never stored, not even briefly for debugging. There is no transcript. What we do keep about asking is two kinds of record, both tied to your anonymous account id and to nothing else about you:
    • a per-day count of how many questions were asked and a running token total, used to enforce fair-use limits; and
    • one log row for each request: when it was made, which model answered, how it ended (answered, empty, error, over the daily limit), how many tokens went in and came out, how many characters and pieces of the answer streamed back, how long each stage took, and, if it failed, a short error note (at most 200 characters). No column in that table can hold the question, the summary, the excerpts or the answer. We read these rows to see whether answers are quick and working; the app never reads them back; they have no automatic expiry today; and they are deleted with your anonymous account when you disconnect Canvas.
  • Your enrollment list (which Canvas courses you're synced to) is kept so the pooling and quota system work, and is deleted if you delete your account.
  • Encrypted sync copies, if you paired the Chrome extension: one encrypted copy per device, replaced each time that device syncs, deleted when you unpair or disconnect Canvas. We hold no key to them.

Deleting your data

Disconnecting Canvas (Profile → accounts → canvas) deletes your class data from our server along with your login on the device: your enrollment records, your usage counters, your per-request log rows (see "What we keep"), and the anonymous account itself, and it removes the Canvas calendar feed address, the saved PennKey password and the Ed Discussion session from this phone, and it unpairs every laptop: their anonymous accounts and encrypted sync copies are deleted too. The confirmation says so before you tap it. If our server can't be reached at that moment, your login still comes off the phone right away, and the app keeps retrying the server-side deletion on later launches until it succeeds (Settings shows "couldn't reach smooth · will retry" meanwhile). If the server refuses the deletion outright, the app stops retrying and says so. It does not delete the shared course material other students are still using, because that material was never yours alone — it's the same syllabus and assignment pages every enrolled student can already see on Canvas. Disconnecting Gradescope removes your Gradescope login and its synced work from this phone.

Deleting the app does not remove everything. iOS removes the app's own storage with it: your list of work and what you've checked off, your grade readings and settings, the announcements list and the widget snapshot. iOS does not remove Keychain items when an app is deleted. If you delete Smooth and install it again on the same phone, these can still be there: your Canvas and Gradescope session cookies, your Canvas calendar feed address, a PennKey password you saved, your Ed Discussion session, the key that signs the phone in to its anonymous account, and a laptop pairing key. Smooth does not clear them when it is installed fresh, so a reinstall is not a clean sign-out. They are kept on this phone only (never in iCloud or on another device), and a saved PennKey password only ever goes to Penn's own login page, as described above. To remove them, disconnect Canvas and Gradescope in Settings before you delete the app. Deleting the app also does not delete anything on our server (only disconnecting Canvas does that) or the copy in your iCloud if you turned sync on (you remove that in iOS Settings, as described under "iCloud sync").

Anonymous accounts

Signing in anonymously means there's no email, password, or name tied to your account — just an id the server uses to tell requests apart for enrollment and quota purposes. One honest limitation: the app currently tells the server which Canvas courses you're in, and the server takes that at face value rather than verifying it against Canvas directly (verifying it would require the server to hold your Canvas login, which we've deliberately built the whole system to avoid). In practice this means a tampered copy of the app could claim membership in a course it isn't enrolled in and read that course's pooled material — material that's already visible to every real student in that course's Canvas site, so nothing personal is exposed this way, but it's worth stating plainly rather than implying a guarantee we haven't built.

What we don't do

  • No analytics, tracking, or advertising, and no data broker or ad network ever receives anything from this app.
  • No third-party SDKs. The app is built directly against Apple's and Supabase's plain HTTP APIs — no analytics kit, ad kit, or crash reporter bundled in.
  • No push notifications. Reminders are scheduled and delivered locally on your device.
  • We don't sell your data. There's nothing to sell: we keep no questions, answers, grades or lists of your work that we can read.
