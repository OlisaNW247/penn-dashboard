# Reading McGraw Hill through Canvas comes first

**Verdict: feasible in two very different senses.** Reading McGraw Hill work that an instructor has already deployed into Canvas is probably feasible today with little or no new code, because McGraw Hill's LTI Advantage integration turns Connect assignments into Canvas assignments with due dates, and Smooth already reads Canvas ([Stonehill guide](https://stonehill.teamdynamix.com/TDClient/1841/Portal/KB/PrintArticle?ID=154634); [CSU Chico](https://support.csuchico.edu/TDClient/1984/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=114811)). That claim is **medium-low confidence until one real Penn Connect section is checked**, and the check costs an afternoon. Reading McGraw Hill directly, either by launching Connect from a Canvas tab in a hidden WebView (the Ed Discussion pattern) or by signing the student into McGraw Hill as Smooth does for Gradescope, is technically plausible but legally exposed: every McGraw Hill terms document surfaced prohibits robots, scrapers and "automatic devices" without written permission, and Apple guideline 5.2.2 requires that a third-party service's terms specifically permit the app's access. I recommend a ranked path: verify and extend the Canvas route first, open an official-partnership conversation in parallel, and treat any direct McGraw Hill reader as gated on written permission and counsel review. **Confidence warning:** the research environment's network policy blocked McGraw Hill's own website, so no McGraw Hill page was read. Every McGraw Hill claim below rests on search-engine summaries of those pages or on third-party school help pages, and is labeled accordingly. **Nothing in this plan has been built or compiled**; the Smooth code seams were read from the repository only, with no Swift toolchain.

## The Canvas route probably already works, with two traps

McGraw Hill's current Canvas integration is LTI Advantage (LTI 1.3), launched from a McGraw Hill tool link in the Canvas course, and an older LTI 1.1 "McGraw-Hill Connect" link is being retired school by school with a migration tool ([McGraw Hill LTIA overview](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/support/knowledge/what-is-lti-advantage-ltia.html); [Boise State migration page](https://talk-boisestate.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LTS/pages/2472050719/McGraw+Hill+Migration+Tool+for+Canvas+LTIA+1.3)). Assignments reach Canvas only when the instructor deploys them, and McGraw Hill treats the Connect due date as the source of truth, edited in Connect rather than Canvas ([McGraw Hill due-date help](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/support/knowledge/connect-set-and-manage-due-dates-for-your-connect-course.html); [LETU service desk](https://servicedesk.letu.edu/TDClient/2460/Portal/KB/Article/121828/How-to-Complete-a-Manual-Sync-between-Connect-and-Canvas)). Canvas shows any published assignment with a due date on its calendar ([Canvas Community](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/Canvas-Question-Forum/Canvas-Calendar-and-Assignments/td-p/539248)), so Smooth's ICS path should already turn a deployed Connect assignment into an ordinary `.canvas` card: the parser keeps only SUMMARY, DTSTART, URL and UID, classifies by URL path then UID, and nothing in the app inspects whether an assignment is an external tool ([CanvasICSClient.swift:36-100](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitKit/Canvas/CanvasICSClient.swift); [ICSParser.swift](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitKit/Canvas/ICSParser.swift)). No source states outright that Connect items appear in the ICS feed or API, so that step is inference.

The first trap is date drift. Embry-Riddle's guide says a grade sync must be rerun after any Connect date change, while another school says dates are inherited, so a Connect-side extension or edit may leave a stale Canvas date ([ERAU](https://trainme.erau.edu/mcgraw-hill-instructor/sync)). Per-student extensions are a Connect feature and no source shows them reaching Canvas ([Connect extensions](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/support/knowledge/connect-assignment-extensions.html)). ALEKS is worse: its Assignments table carries per-student "Exceptions," and in Canvas it syncs either one total-grade column or per-assignment items depending on instructor setup, with no documented due dates for the Canvas items ([ALEKS student view](https://www.mheducation.com/support/aleks-support-center/knowledge/aleks-assignments-student-view.html); [Rutgers ALEKS guide](https://canvas.rutgers.edu/documentation/external-apps/aleks-getting-started-for-instructors/)). SIMnet's integration explicitly does not support due-date changes ([SIMnet](https://help.simnetonline.com/manager/integrations/lti-advantage-v1.3)). Link-only or inclusive-access courses create nothing in Canvas at all.

The second trap is the "done" state, and it is where the codebase reading earns its keep. McGraw Hill sends an automatic zero to the LMS at the due date for work a student never completed ([Grade Sync troubleshooting](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/support/knowledge/grade-sync-troubleshoot-grade-sync-in-lti-advantage.html)). Smooth's `indicatesSubmitted` does not treat a bare score as submission: it requires `submitted_at`, or workflow state `submitted` or `pending_review`, and counts `graded` only with a submitted timestamp ([CanvasGradesClient.swift:400-412](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitKit/Canvas/CanvasGradesClient.swift)), so auto-zeros will not falsely mark work done. The opposite failure is the live risk: whether Canvas ever records `submitted_at` for a Connect completion before the due date is undocumented. If it does not, a student who finished a Connect set still sees an overdue card, because `external_tool` assignments count as "expects a submission" ([GradeModels.swift:47-81](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitKit/Models/GradeModels.swift)), and the Smooth Sweeper alarm, which rings ten minutes before unsubmitted work, would fire for work already done ([NotificationScheduler.swift:796](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitUI/NotificationScheduler.swift)). That is a product-quality problem Smooth could create by merely succeeding at Tier 0, which is why the experiments below come before any marketing of the feature.

## Four options ranked by value, risk and cost

| Rank | Option | What it gives | Main risk | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Canvas-native reading (assignments already in Canvas) | Class-level due dates for deployed Connect/ALEKS items; Canvas link to launch | Stale dates, no per-student extensions, uncertain done-state | Medium-low until tested |
| 2 | Official partnership or Penn-sponsored access | Permission for anything deeper; answers Apple 5.2.2 | Slow; no public partner program found | Low on feasibility |
| 3 | Hidden-WebView LTI launch (Ed pattern) | Student-level state from Connect's own app | Terms clause; launch opens a new tab; short-lived tokens | Low-medium |
| 4 | Direct McGraw Hill login (Gradescope pattern) | Works without LMS; only route to ALEKS exceptions | Terms clause; stored credential; likely CAPTCHA | Low |

Option 1 wins on every axis except completeness, and its limits are exactly what would justify the others. Option 2 ranks second because it is the only thing that converts options 3 and 4 from a contract violation into a licensed feature. The research found no public developer API, partner portal or student-app program, only institution-run LTI 1.3 with grade sync, so the realistic entry is McGraw Hill higher-ed business development or a Penn instructional-technology sponsor ([LTI Advantage press release](https://www.mheducation.com/news-media/press-releases/mcgraw-hill-lti-advantage-connect-integrations.html)). Peers offer no precedent to copy: Pearson and Cengage document due-date delivery into the LMS and a first-party student app, not third-party APIs ([Pearson](https://help.pearsoncmg.com/integration/cg/instructor/content/sync_grades.htm); [Cengage](https://help.cengage.com/mindtap/mt-instructor/common/lms-sync-dates-general.html)).

Option 3 ranks above option 4 because it needs no new credential. A paired student reaches Connect from Canvas without typing a McGraw Hill password, and Smooth already holds the Canvas session in the Keychain and injects it into a hidden WebView before a single launch, exactly as `EdSessionLauncher` does ([McGraw Hill pairing guide](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/support/knowledge/connect-how-to-pair-an-account-in-canvas-as-a-student.html); [EdSessionLauncher.swift:104-221](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitUI/EdSessionLauncher.swift)). The technical evidence for what comes next is a single hobbyist planner project, schoolhub, whose code comment says the long-lived `ERIGHTS` cookie on `newconnect.mheducation.com` mints a five-minute `MH_TOKEN` JWT via a `refreshToken` call, which a bearer-authenticated assignments API then accepts; it claims live verification on 2026-08-25 ([schoolhub sites.py](https://github.com/mrsoftskin/schoolhub/blob/main/src/brain/connectors/sites.py)). That is one author's claim read as search fragments; the assignments endpoint path and JSON schema were never recovered, and must be treated as unknown. Two further unknowns could break the Ed analogy: whether McGraw Hill's launch needs a new window or a user gesture, which a hidden WebView may not satisfy (unverified inference; Ed's launch needed only the injected Canvas cookies), and that Ed's token lived in `localStorage` while Connect's reportedly lives in a cookie, so the token-read step would be rewritten, not reused.

Option 4 is the Gradescope shape: a visible login pane on its own persistent data store, cookies in a per-service Keychain item, a hand-built `Cookie` header, and a reauth rule that never disconnects on a failure ([OnboardingView.swift:1329-1404](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitUI/OnboardingView.swift); [GradescopeClient.swift:223-261](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitKit/Gradescope/GradescopeClient.swift)). Its distinct value is ALEKS, which has its own `aleks.com/login` and password and whose per-student exceptions Canvas cannot show ([InterALEKS source, login target](https://github.com/LaganYT/InterALEKS/blob/main/content.js)). Its costs are a new stored credential class, a likely bot challenge (reCAPTCHA appears in tracker catalogs, unverified for the login page), and the same terms exposure as option 3 with a more visible footprint. The only phone surface McGraw Hill itself offers, ReadAnywhere, is an eBook and SmartBook reader with no evidence of a due-date list, so a Smooth to-do feed would fill a real gap ([ReadAnywhere](https://www.mheducation.com/readanywhere.html)).

## Terms, Apple and the Power Ventures problem decide what ships

**McGraw Hill's terms (reported by search summary, not read).** Summaries of McGraw Hill's US terms limit use to "your own internal use for educational purposes only," and a summarized clause bars "any robot, spider, agent, artificial intelligence (AI), other automatic device or program or manual process to access, monitor, scrape, crawl, data mine, harvest, frame or mirror, deep link"; the international terms bar password sharing, and a customer-portal document bars bots and scrapers ([US terms](https://www.mheducation.com/about-us/policy-center/terms-use.html); [international terms](https://www.mheducation.com/about-us/policy-center/terms-use/international); [portal terms](https://icss.mheducation.com/static/termsofuse)). The wording and effective dates conflict across sources and the AI-agent language is unconfirmed, so the first legal task is to read the live pages. Smooth's on-device design means it is not sharing a password with a server, but a reader acting on a student's account is plausibly an "automatic program" under this clause, and the student accepted these terms when registering ([UToledo ALEKS registration](https://www.utoledo.edu/it/aleks/ALEKS_Student_Registration_and_Access_Code_Purchase_New_Instructions.docx)). Option 1 sidesteps the clause entirely, because Smooth then reads only Canvas.

**Apple 5.2.2.** Apple's guidelines, read directly and dated 16 October 2026, say an app that "uses, accesses, monetizes access to, or displays content from a third-party service" must be "specifically permitted to do so under the service's terms of use," with authorization provided on request ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). If the terms bar automated access, Smooth cannot meet that for McGraw Hill without written permission. Guideline 5.1.1(v)'s principle of credentials staying on-device and used only while the app is in use matches Smooth's design, and 5.1.2 requires disclosure of any AI-related third-party sharing, so McGraw Hill data must stay out of the `ask` context; `assistantSourceLabel` is a deliberately default-less switch that forces that decision ([AssistantContextAssembly.swift:219-234](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitUI/AssistantContextAssembly.swift)). No data exists on how strictly 5.2.2 is enforced against login-and-read apps, though Smooth's existing Canvas and Gradescope approvals say nothing about McGraw Hill's terms.

**CFAA and revocation.** The case law is mixed. *Facebook v. Power Ventures* held that user permission did not survive Facebook's explicit cease-and-desist and IP block, so continued access violated the CFAA, while a terms violation alone was not enough ([Cato brief](https://www.cato.org/publications/legal-briefs/power-ventures-v-facebook); [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2826/2016-07-facebook-v-vachani-user-authorization-can-be-revoked)). *hiQ v. LinkedIn* and *Meta v. Bright Data* favored scrapers, but both concerned public or logged-off data and Bright Data turned on Meta's specific wording ([Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/taking-cue-from-the-supreme-courts-van-buren-decision-ninth-circuit-releases-new-opinion-holding-scraping-of-publicly-available-website-data-falls-outside-of-cfaa); [FBM](https://www.fbm.com/data-analytics/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/)). Smooth's logged-in, private, user-authorized facts sit nearest Power Ventures, which means the working rule is a kill switch: if McGraw Hill ever sends an objection, the McGraw Hill reader must be switchable off without a release, as `FeatureFlags.edDiscussion` is ([FeatureFlags.swift:84-104](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitUI/FeatureFlags.swift)). The primary opinions were not read; these are law-firm summaries and none of this is legal advice.

**Enforcement evidence is thin but not reassuring in either direction.** A search of GitHub's public DMCA repository found about 56 McGraw Hill notices from 2015 to April 2026, all aimed at pirated textbook PDFs, none at Connect, ALEKS or any tool that reads them ([github/dmca](https://github.com/github/dmca)). Cease-and-desist letters and store complaints are not public, so absence is weak evidence. FERPA is probably not the binding constraint, since a student reading their own records on their own phone involves no school disclosure to Smooth, but any server-side relay of McGraw Hill data would change that analysis and Smooth's privacy posture ([Dept. of Education](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/faq/who-school-official-under-ferpa)). Penn's own posture is restrictive toward third-party access: student Canvas API tokens have been disabled since 2025, which is why Smooth uses the web session ([Penn Canvas policies, via search summary](https://infocanvas.upenn.edu/policies/canvas-site-policies)).

## A phased plan against Smooth's real seams

**Phase 0: settle the unknowns, no code shipped (days).** Run the experiments in the next section. Their results decide whether Phase 1 is worth building and whether Phases 3 and 4 are needed at all. Re-fetch the blocked McGraw Hill pages (terms, LTIA instructor guide, Grade Sync article) once the network allows, and log the verbatim restriction clause.

**Phase 1: make Canvas-sourced McGraw Hill work first-class (small).** Extend the assignment DTO in `CanvasGradesClient` to decode `external_tool_tag_attributes` and `html_url` ([CanvasGradesClient.swift:497-531](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitKit/Canvas/CanvasGradesClient.swift)), so a Canvas assignment can be identified as a McGraw Hill launch by host. Use that to do three things without adding a source: label the card "in connect" or "in aleks," keep the "open in canvas" link (which reaches the LTI launch), and, if Phase 0 shows Canvas never records completion, mark identified items as "completion not visible" so the swipe is the student's tool and the Sweeper does not alarm for them. Because Phase 1 adds no row, it avoids the source-identity problem entirely: the code warns that a new `Assignment.Source` value would show a newer build's rows to an older build as Canvas items with mismatched ids, and a new column is a SwiftData schema change ([AnnouncementLog.swift:343-358](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitKit/Canvas/AnnouncementLog.swift)). Add the fixture Smooth lacks today: no test models an LTI assignment. ICS carries no description field, so nothing beyond the URL is available there ([ICSParser.swift](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitKit/Canvas/ICSParser.swift)).

**Phase 2: the gate (weeks, mostly people).** Send an outreach to McGraw Hill higher-ed partnerships and to Penn instructional technology asking for written permission or a sponsored pilot, and have counsel read the live terms. No Phase 3 or 4 code ships to the App Store without either written permission or an explicit, documented decision by the owners to accept the risk with a kill switch. This gate belongs to Olisa and Marco, not to an implementer.

**Phase 3: Connect through the Canvas session (medium, only if the gate passes and Phase 0 shows a student-level gap).** Reuse the Ed scaffolding: discover the McGraw Hill tool from `/tabs` with a matcher in the shape of `EdTabFinder`, and from module `ExternalTool` items, which today yield `url: nil` ([CanvasCourseContentClient.swift:93-107](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitKit/CanvasAPI/CanvasCourseContentClient.swift); [EdDiscussionProbe.swift:29-87](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitKit/Ed/EdDiscussionProbe.swift); [AppState.swift:5070-5105](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitUI/AppState.swift)). Launch in a hidden WebView on `LoginDataStores.canvas` with injected Canvas cookies, one load, a settle wait and a 40-second cap, then read the resulting `.mheducation.com` cookies instead of `localStorage` ([EdSessionLauncher.swift:104-221](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitUI/EdSessionLauncher.swift)). Add a Keychain service for those cookies beside the Ed cases, a JSON client in the `EdClient` style that mints the short-lived token on every poll, and a reauth rule modeled on `GradescopeReauthRule` that believes only a login page ([SessionCookieStore.swift:24-47](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitUI/SessionCookieStore.swift); [GradescopeReauthRule.swift](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitKit/Gradescope/GradescopeReauthRule.swift)). Results enrich the existing Canvas row (submitted state, the student's own due date) rather than creating a third row, since `AssignmentDeduplicator` is hard-wired to two sides and `Match` holds one `linkedID` ([AssignmentDeduplicator.swift:29-36](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitKit/Models/AssignmentDeduplicator.swift)). Keep it on-device and not Penn-gated, because the Penn gate on Ed exists only for server pooling keyed by numeric Canvas course ids, and McGraw Hill data should never be pooled ([AppState+CourseKnowledge.swift:498-525](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitUI/AppState+CourseKnowledge.swift)). Ship behind a new `FeatureFlags` kill switch, default off.

**Phase 4: ALEKS direct login (only for ALEKS, last).** A Gradescope-shaped pane on `www.aleks.com/login` with a new `Service` case, a fixed-UUID entry in `LoginDataStores`, an `OnboardingView.Phase` and `OnboardingDestination` case, a third row and `DisconnectTarget` in Profile → accounts, and a parser for a server-rendered table whose structure is unknown ([LoginDataStores.swift:26-50](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitUI/LoginDataStores.swift); [SettingsPage.swift:69-85, 131-157](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitUI/SettingsPage.swift)). No scraper or endpoint evidence for ALEKS exists, so this phase starts with reconnaissance on a real account.

**Cross-cutting duties for Phases 3 and 4.** Update `docs/PRIVACY.md` in the same commit as any behavior, as the repository's own rule requires, and re-read the App Store privacy label and review notes; the Ed section's wording about a session obtained through another service's login is the template ([PRIVACY.md:163-173](docs/PRIVACY.md)). Keep the feature absent from preview and demo mode, App Review's only way in. By extrapolation from line counts, a Gradescope-shaped integration is roughly 1,500 to 2,500 lines of Swift with 100 to 200 tests touching about 25 to 30 files, and an Ed-shaped one roughly 3,500 to 5,000 lines with 150-plus tests; the long tail of reauth semantics and privacy copy, not the happy path, has been the real cost in both precedents. Every line of that is unwritten, and any of it must compile on a Mac before it counts.

## Five cheap experiments settle the biggest unknowns

The single most valuable test is a real Penn Connect section viewed through the Canvas API with a student's session. Call `GET /api/v1/courses/:id/assignments?include[]=submission` and record, per Connect item: `submission_types` (expect `external_tool`), `external_tool_tag_attributes.url` and its host (no source names it), `due_at`, `html_url`, and `submission.workflow_state` and `submitted_at`. Capture it at three moments for one item: before the student starts, after the student completes it but before the due date, and after the due date passes, since McGraw Hill's zero is posted at the due date and nothing documents when non-zero scores post. A DEBUG build's `submissionDiagnosticLines` can read this off a phone without new code.

The second test is the calendar feed: pull the course's ICS feed and record the UID, URL and SUMMARY of the Connect event, since the repository has no fixture for an external-tool event and `classifyFromURL` returns nil for the `/calendar?...#assignment_<id>` shape, leaving the UID fallback to decide the kind ([CanvasICSClient.swift:62-100](LowHangingFruitKit/Sources/LowHangingFruitKit/Canvas/CanvasICSClient.swift)). The third is date propagation with a cooperating instructor: change a due date in Connect, then record when and whether the Canvas date moves, and repeat with a per-student extension. The fourth is population: list the `external_tool_tag_attributes.url` hosts across the server's pooled assignment documents or a few volunteers' Canvas data to find which Penn courses use Connect or ALEKS at all, since no Penn syllabus naming either was found and the Mandel economics text is only a weak hint ([McGraw Hill product page](https://mheducation.com/highered/product/m-economics-basics/M9781260226768.html)). The fifth is the tool version and mode at Penn: whether sections are on LTI 1.1 or 1.3, and whether ALEKS courses sync a total column or per-assignment items. Only if these show a student-level gap that Canvas cannot fill is Phase 3 worth the legal cost.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "can Smooth log into McGraw Hill" to "how much of McGraw Hill is already in Canvas, and is the missing remainder worth a contract fight." The cheapest and safest path is also the one Smooth is closest to, and its likeliest failure, a card that stays overdue after a student finishes a Connect set, is a bug Smooth would create rather than a limit McGraw Hill imposes, which makes Phase 1's "completion not visible" handling more urgent than any new login. The unusual lesson from Smooth's own history is that its sign-in trust has always rested on being invisible and permitted, and McGraw Hill is the first source where the terms say otherwise; a reader built on schoolhub-style cookie minting should therefore be treated as a prototype to hold behind a flag until written permission arrives, not a feature to schedule.

What remains unknown is large and mostly empirical: McGraw Hill's live terms text, the assignments endpoint schema, ERIGHTS cookie lifetime, bot defenses on `accounts.mheducation.com`, and Penn's adoption rate. None of those were reachable from this research environment, so every McGraw Hill-specific statement above should be re-verified against primary pages and a real Penn account before any decision depends on it.
