## 1. Duolingo: the 2022 move from the skill "tree" to the single "path" (what changed, why, results, complaints; streaks, daily goals, units/sections, jump-ahead, done / current / locked)

### Takeaway
Duolingo replaced a branching tree, where the learner chose which skill to do and how deep to take it, with one linear path of small circles followed step by step, with review, stories and practice folded into the same line. Its stated reasons were learner confusion ("am I doing this the right way?") and learning science (spaced repetition by interleaving); the company's CEO said engagement was equivalent and refused to revert, while a vocal group of existing users petitioned against the loss of choice.

### Cited Findings

**What changed**
- The home screen became "a path that you'll follow step by step"; under the old design lessons were grouped into skills, and the new design interleaves them along a single path. Launched to all learners on November 1, 2022 (per search excerpt of the post). — [Duolingo blog, "The Science Behind Duolingo's Home Screen Redesign"](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/)
- Duolingo's shareholder letter described the change as the learning "tree" being replaced by "a simpler 'path' that guides learners through their course". Lesson content stayed the same but was reordered to mix concepts. Each circle on the path equals one crown level of the old skill system; lessons are grouped into smaller units; practice is built into the path so learners do not have to go back to "cracked" skills; tips moved into a "guidebook" per unit; Stories moved from a separate tab into the path. — [Duolingo shareholder letter (title in results: "Q1 highlights 9.5M 12.5M", which looks like the Q1 2022 letter; quarter not confirmed)](https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/d667f9d1-8f7f-418c-b116-e699a30a9826)
- Before the path, after the 2018 "Crown Levels" update, learners "no longer explicitly leveraged spaced repetition at the individual skill level"; Duolingo's patch at the time was to make a finished (Level 5) skill show as broken when its algorithm said it was time to review. The path later removed the need for that by scheduling review itself. — [Duolingo blog, 2019/2020 learning-experience recap](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-weve-improved-the-duolingo-learning-experience-this-year-and-a-sneak-peek-toward-2020/)

**Why Duolingo said it did it**
- Learning science: "the ordering of lessons in the path is grounded in spaced repetition", and the learner sees "a mix of lessons, with some that cover brand-new concepts and some that cover previously introduced concepts you need to review". Practice sessions were built into the path so learners regularly revisit material. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/)
- Confusion: the redesign was "meant to simplify things for new users who were sometimes confused about how to best make progress on Duolingo". — [Duolingo shareholder letter](https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/d667f9d1-8f7f-418c-b116-e699a30a9826)
- A Duolingo spokesperson: "We often heard from learners that they're not sure whether they're using Duolingo in the 'correct' or 'best' way." (The search excerpt did not say which outlet carried the quote; it was among the press results for the redesign.) — [Yahoo Finance UK / PA, "biggest ever redesign"](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/duolingo-language-learning-app-undergoes-135344395.html); [Silicon Republic](https://www.siliconrepublic.com/?p=988615)
- CEO Luis von Ahn's stated goals were "decreasing confusion and increasing learning outcomes". — [NBC News interview](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/duolingos-update-redesign-luis-von-ahn-interview-rcna44655)

**Results Duolingo published or stated**
- Von Ahn told NBC News he has no plans to undo any of the changes; internal metrics indicated equivalent engagement with the new interface compared with the old one. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/duolingos-update-redesign-luis-von-ahn-interview-rcna44655); syndicated as ["Duolingo's redesign has some fans up in arms. Its CEO says it's not turning back."](https://news.yahoo.com/duolingo-redesign-fans-arms-ceo-204849479.html)
- The rollout went to new users first because the company expected pushback from existing ones; that let it gauge engagement and collect early input. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/duolingos-update-redesign-luis-von-ahn-interview-rcna44655)
- A fan wiki says von Ahn claimed daily active users increased in the new layout. Secondary, unconfirmed against a primary source. — [Duolingo fandom wiki, "Language tree"](https://duolingo.fandom.com/wiki/Language_tree)

**What users complained about**
- Loss of choice: the path "removed choice from the Duolingo menu", placing every user on a predetermined, uniform lesson plan. — [Medium / Bootcamp, "Duo's Cage I: Duolingo's New Learning Path"](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/duos-cage-part-i-80ec44f4ab45)
- A Change.org petition to reverse the path update showed roughly 15,000 to 16,000 signatures in later snapshots (counts differ between pages), with complaints continuing "several months AFTER the update". A second petition is titled "No, you are not an apple: reinstate the non linear learning path on Duolingo". — [Change.org, "Reverse duolingo's path update"](https://www.change.org/p/reverse-duolingo-s-path-update); [Change.org, "reinstate the non linear learning path"](https://www.change.org/p/no-you-are-not-an-apple-reinstate-the-non-linear-learning-path-on-duolingo)
- A later failure of a single line: when Duolingo updates a course's content, learners' positions on the path are remapped, and Android Authority reported many users "stranded" on their paths after course updates. — [Android Authority, "why I quit"](https://androidauthority.com/duolingo-broken-updated-courses-why-i-quit-3686405); [Android Authority poll](https://www.androidauthority.com/duolingo-course-updates-poll-results-3690803/)

**Structure of the path: sections, units, nodes**
- The path is divided into sections, and sections into units. Tapping the section label opens the list of units in that section; an "X" at the top left closes the list and returns "to your current place in the learning path". — [Duolingo blog, via search excerpt](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-to-review-lessons-on-duolingo)
- A unit is a short series of nodes with different symbols by activity type: Duolingo describes "a series of eight nodes with different symbols (a star, a book, a video camera, headphones, etc.)". Intermediate learners (Duolingo Score 60 to 130) get shorter "mini-units"; a 2025 recap says units for learners above Score 60 are broken into smaller "chunks". — [Duolingo blog, mini-units](https://blog.duolingo.com/intermediate-mini-units/); [Duolingo blog, 2025 highlights](https://blog.duolingo.com/product-highlights/)
- Third-party count: each section of the Spanish course holds 28 to 55 units. — [Lumetest (third party)](https://lumetest.com/blog/det/how-many-sections-in-duolingo-spanish-complete-course-structure-guide)

**Done / gold / jump ahead**
- A finished node can be upgraded: "When you've completed a node, select the Legendary option to do a lesson with more challenging exercises and no hints ... the node will turn gold afterwards!" Duolingo's blog refers to "a completed (gold) level in your path". — [Duolingo blog, ways to practice](https://blog.duolingo.com/ways-to-practice-in-duolingo)
- Jump ahead: the learner scrolls down the path to a later unit and taps a "Jump Here?" button; the app asks to confirm and then gives a test "to see if you're ready". (Fan blog; wording may have changed.) — [Happily Ever Travels (fan blog)](https://happilyevertravels.com/?p=4152)

**Streaks and daily goals (published experiment results)**
- Separating the daily goal from the streak (so a streak can be extended by one lesson without hitting the XP goal): +3.3% Day-14 retention, +1% overall daily active learners, +10.5% in the share of daily learners on a streak (figures as reported, all relative). — [Duolingo blog, "Improving the streak"](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)
- Streak Wager (betting gems on keeping a streak): Day-1, Day-7 and Day-14 retention all rose, Day-7 the most at +14%. — [Making Duolingo, "How streaks keep Duolingo learners committed"](https://making.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals)
- Streak Freeze lets a learner protect a streak for a missed day; up to two can be equipped at once. No retention figure for the freeze itself was found in a primary source; a portfolio case study's "21% churn reduction" is unsourced. — [Duolingo blog, "How the Duolingo streak builds habit"](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)
- Streak-goal screen: a former Duolingo retention-team member wrote that removing the preselected goal and making the learner choose one led users to pick higher goals, not drop off despite the extra tap, and "retain way better for weeks after seeing the screen"; the chosen goal was not even stored at the time. — [Ali Abouelatta thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1594707015032455168)

### Inferences
- The path trades choice for certainty about "what do I do next". Duolingo's own justification is not that the tree was ugly, but that a learner facing many open nodes could not tell which was the right one. A degree audit with ten open requirement cards poses the same question.
- The path absorbed things that used to be separate places (Stories tab, practice of cracked skills, tips). Part of the simplification is fewer destinations, not just a different drawing.
- The backlash was concentrated among existing, advanced users who had a mental model and a strategy under the old layout; Duolingo anticipated that and tested on new users first. A redesign for a feature with one or two real users today does not carry that cost, but "let me see everything and pick" is a real need for expert users and Duolingo kept a narrow version of it (section list, jump-ahead test).
- The Android Authority "stranded" reports are a warning for any single-line progress display whose underlying content can change: when the source of truth is rearranged (a course update there; a re-read audit or a what-if here), a position on a line can jump, and users notice.
- The streak-goal result suggests that asking a person to commit to a target themselves, one screen, one choice, changes later behaviour even when nothing else changes.

### Gaps
- I could not read Duolingo's blog post in full, so I have no verbatim paragraph-length quote, and no confirmation of whether it cites an A/B-test learning-outcome result for the path. The only quantitative statement found is von Ahn's "equivalent engagement" via NBC.
- No source found that describes exactly how the path draws current versus locked nodes. Unsourced, from general knowledge of the app: completed nodes are filled in the unit's colour (gold if Legendary), the current node is raised with a progress ring around it and a "START" callout, later nodes are grey; tapping a grey node offers the jump-ahead test rather than nothing. Verify in the app before relying on it.
- Unsourced: the daily goal is chosen at onboarding as minutes or XP per day with labels like casual / regular / serious / intense. Not confirmed in this session.
- The fan-wiki claim that DAU rose under the path is unconfirmed; Duolingo's DAU rose strongly through 2022 to 2023 overall, and no source here isolates the path's contribution.

## 2. Apple Fitness activity rings: why three, why legible at a glance, overshoot, critiques

### Takeaway
The rings replace numbers with a shape whose only question is "is it closed?": three concentric rings in three fixed colours, one daily goal ("close them"). Apple's fitness lead said the design was built around a visual precisely because other trackers led with numbers, and it tested well across athletes and beginners alike; critics say the three chosen metrics are dated, that daily closure ignores rest and weekly consistency, and one review of studies reports that bars are read faster and more accurately than rings.

### Cited Findings
- Apple's own pitch: "Three rings: Move, Exercise, Stand. One goal: Close them every day." — [Apple, Close Your Rings](https://www.apple.com/om/watch/close-your-rings/)
- "Activity rings show an individual's daily progress toward Move, Exercise, and Stand goals." In watchOS the element "always contains three rings, whose colors and meanings match those the Activity app provides"; on an iPhone with no watch paired only a single Move ring is shown. (Read in full.) — [Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Activity rings](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/activity-rings.md)
- Apple protects the rings' meaning as a fixed vocabulary: "Activity rings are designed to consistently represent progress in these specific areas. Don't replicate or modify Activity rings for other purposes. Never use Activity rings to display other types of data." And: "Never use Activity rings to represent data for more than one person". And: "Mixing different ring styles can lead to a visually confusing interface." (Read in full.) — [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/activity-rings.md)
- Legibility rules Apple enforces: never change the ring colours; always a black background; keep a margin "no less than the distance between rings"; labels or values tied to a ring use that ring's exact colour (Move red R250 G17 B79, Exercise green R166 G255 B0, Stand blue R0 G255 B246). (Read in full.) — [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/activity-rings.md)
- Jay Blahnik (Apple's fitness director): typical trackers "also measure metrics but they tend to do them in numbers", while Apple "built the entire design around a visual". Apple first planned separate experiences for serious athletes and casual exercisers, then found the rings were "resonant among all users". He contrasted three self-set goals with the single 10,000-steps paradigm. — [MobiHealthNews, "Jay Blahnik: what separates Apple Watch from other fitness trackers"](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/content/jay-blahnik-what-separates-apple-watch-other-fitness-trackers); [MobiHealthNews, behaviour change interview](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/apples-fitness-director-talks-apple-watch-behavior-change-future-apps)
- Apple's design process, as reported by Engadget in 2015, started from asking what could measure all-day activity more usefully than a step count. (Not read in full; no detail on rejected alternatives recovered.) — [Engadget, "Apple labored over the perfect interface for its Watch Activity app"](https://www.engadget.com/2015-03-10-apple-watch-activity-design.html)
- Critique, metrics: Macworld argues the three-ring scheme needs updating; experts quoted suggest strength, mobility, recovery and sleep, weekly consistency over daily streaks, and letting users define a custom ring. — [Macworld, "Ring toss: why the Apple Watch activity goals need an update"](https://www.macworld.com/article/3183369/ring-toss-why-the-apple-watch-activity-goals-need-an-update.html)
- Critique, glanceability has a cost: a 2026 media-studies article argues designing for brief glances forces a balance of simplicity against complexity, that small screens restrict nuance about personal health, and that the per-activity screens fragment the data. — [Schmid, "Glanceability and voyeuristic distance", Convergence (SAGE), 2026](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/13548565261442032)
- Critique, encoding: a report on a review of 51 studies says bar charts were read faster and with fewer mistakes than radial rings in goal-progress tasks, although people found rings more attractive. Secondary summary; the underlying review was not located. — [Quest Review Center](https://questreviewcenter.com/product-reviews/smartwatch-activity-rings-look-appealing-but-bars-make-progress-easier-to-read/)
- Critique, mismatch: the Exercise ring tracks cardio well but not strength work or manual labour, so a hard day can leave it unchanged; and a behavioural-psychology reviewer says the absence of gradual "shaping" steps can discourage beginners. — [BehaviorFit review](https://behaviorfit.com/behaviorfitapplewatchfitness/)
- An app markets itself as the rings' replacement on the grounds of rest and recovery. (Title only; content not read.) — [Gentler Streak article](https://blog.dion.software/articles/gentler-streak)

### Inferences
- Three is not explained by Apple anywhere I found. What the sources do support: (a) one metric (steps) was judged too thin, (b) the three rings measure different kinds of thing (total energy, intensity, not sitting), and (c) all three collapse into one instruction, "close them". The count is small enough to be learned once and never re-read.
- The rings carry no numbers at a glance. The numbers exist one tap in. The first read is binary per ring (closed or not) and the second read is "how far round".
- Apple's HIG rule against two ring styles on one screen, and against one ring set for two people, is directly relevant to a dual-degree screen: two sets of similar rings side by side is a pattern Apple itself forbids as confusing.
- The bars-versus-rings finding (if the underlying review holds) says rings are chosen for appeal and identity, not for reading precision. A ring is a good "am I done" mark and a poor "how many are left" mark.
- A daily ring resets; a degree does not. The rings' motivational pull comes from the daily reset and the streak, which a four-year progress figure cannot borrow.

### Gaps
- How overshoot is drawn: no source found. Unsourced, from general knowledge: when a goal is exceeded the ring keeps going and laps over itself, with a small shadow under the leading end so the overlap is visible, and the percentage in the detail view exceeds 100; double and triple laps are possible. Verify before relying on it.
- Why exactly three rings (and not two or four): no primary statement found.
- The BehaviorFit claim that only the Move goal is customisable may be outdated (unsourced: later watchOS versions let users change Exercise and Stand goals and schedule different goals by weekday, and pause rings). Not confirmed here.
- The Engadget piece on Apple's design memo could not be read, so rejected alternatives and designer quotes are missing.

## 3. Khan Academy mastery, Brilliant, Headspace / Calm: a long course as a small number of steps

### Takeaway
These products compress a long course into a short ladder of named states and one headline figure. Khan Academy uses four named levels per skill and a single course percentage that counts only skills at the top two levels; Brilliant and Headspace use an ordered list of a few named steps with the current one on top. Khan's recent change away from the course-wide percent toward counts inside each unit shows the single big percent is not always the right headline.

### Cited Findings
- Khan Academy skill levels, in order: Attempted, Familiar, Proficient, Mastered. "Familiar: You've demonstrated some understanding of the skill. Proficient: You've demonstrated proficiency in the skill. Mastered: You've demonstrated mastery of the skill." — [Khan Academy help, "How do Khan Academy's Mastery levels work?"](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/tr/articles/5548760867853)
- The course mastery percentage is the share of the course's skills currently at Proficient or Mastered; Not started, Attempted and Familiar count for nothing. Example given: 20 skills, 5 at Proficient or Mastered, 25%. — [Khan Academy help, "How is my mastery percentage calculated?"](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/48411237993357-How-is-my-mastery-percentage-calculated)
- In Khan Academy's "reimagined" experience course-level percentages are no longer shown; progress is measured by the number of skills that reach Proficient or higher within each Unit Mission. The classic experience still shows course mastery percent for self-directed learners. — [Khan Academy help, "Update: How mastery percentage is calculated"](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/48431088309645-Update-How-mastery-percentage-is-calculated-on-Khan-Academy)
- Khan Academy chose "skills to proficient" as its outcome measure and argued others should too. (Title and summary only.) — [Khan Academy blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=13201)
- Levels can go down as well as up: under the September 2018 mechanics a Proficient skill dropped to Familiar on anything short of a perfect score. May be outdated. — [Khan Academy community update, Sept 2018](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/de/community/posts/360023249611/comments/360003019811)
- Brilliant's vocabulary is three nested words: topics (formerly courses), units (formerly levels), concepts (formerly lessons); progress carried over in the rename. Learning Paths sit on the Topics page; free users must take the concepts of a topic in sequence, premium users can take topics in any order. — [Brilliant help, "What are learning paths?"](https://brilliant.org/help/features/what-are-learning-paths); [Brilliant help, "Why did the content change?"](https://brilliant.org/help/help-and-support/why-did-the-content-change/)
- A Brilliant user's complaint that matters for any progress display: the progress bar for a course "was no longer consistent across the UI", showing different amounts in different places. — [Ben Wheatley blog, 2024](https://benwheatley.github.io/blog/2024/03/11-12.00.16.html)
- Headspace: packs "are arranged in an ascending timeline with your current pack at the top". Its Pro content is numbered levels (pages exist for "Pro | Level 4" and "Pro | Level 8"), and its employer guide is organised as weekly themes (Week 1 building your practice, Week 2 stress and anxiety, Week 3 loneliness). — [Business Insider slideshow](https://www.businessinsider.in/I've-used-a-meditation-app-loved-by-Wall-Streeters-and-Olympic-athletes-daily-for-the-past-8-months-and-now-I-can't-imagine-my-life-without-it/Your-packs-are-arranged-in-an-ascending-timeline-with-your-current-pack-at-the-top-/slideshow/53802288.cms); [Headspace, Pro Level 8](https://www.headspace.com/content/course/pro--level-8/1015); [Headspace weekly practice guide (UVM copy)](https://www.uvm.edu/sites/default/files/Employee-Wellness/2022/headspace%20resources/Headspace_WeeklyPracticeGuide.pdf)
- Peloton programs state their whole shape up front in two numbers, e.g. "5 weeks long and includes 3 classes per week", and are gated: classes are unlocked in chronological order. — [Peloton blog, programs](https://www.onepeloton.com/blog/peloton-programs); [Pelobuddy](https://www.pelobuddy.com/?p=41945)

### Inferences
- Khan's percent has a strict rule (only the top two levels count) that makes the number conservative and stable: partial credit is shown as a colour on the skill, never as a fraction of a percent. That keeps the headline from moving for things that are merely started.
- Khan moving the headline from "course percent" to "skills at proficient within this unit" suggests that for long courses a nearby, countable target ("7 of 12 in this unit") motivates better than a distant percentage that moves one point at a time.
- The Brilliant complaint is the direct analogue of a degree screen that shows Penn's percent, an app estimate with plan, and a by-target figure: when the same progress shows as different numbers in different places, users read it as a bug.
- Every one of these names its levels with words (Familiar, Proficient, Mastered; Level 4; Week 2) so the state can be said aloud. None relies on colour alone.

### Gaps
- Calm: no source found on how Calm presents multi-day courses.
- Headspace's current (2025 to 2026) course screen was not found; the Business Insider description is from about 2016.
- Brilliant's home screen layout (what is first on screen) was not described by any source found.
- No published data from Khan Academy on whether the unit-count display outperforms the course percent.

## 4. Training-plan apps (Strava, Nike Run Club, Couch to 5K, Peloton, Runna): a multi-week plan, the current week, and falling behind

### Takeaway
Training plans show a long goal as weeks, and a week as a short list of sessions; the plan's two headline facts are its length and its end date. The best-documented handling of falling behind is Runna's: after a missed week it stops and asks one question with three plain choices (skip what was missed, move it later, or carry on), and it distinguishes "skip" from "move". The general coaching rule across sources is to skip and continue, not to cram.

### Cited Findings
- NHS Couch to 5K states the whole plan in one sentence: "over the next 9 weeks, you'll run 3 times a week, alternating between walking and running". The app gives audio coaching, tracks progress and times the runs. — [NHS, Couch to 5K running plan](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/couch-to-5k-running-plan)
- Couch to 5K has a different schedule for each of the nine weeks, three runs with a rest day between; a charity guide notes two runs is acceptable in the first four weeks, when each run in a week is the same. — [WCRF beginner's guide](https://www.wcrf.org/about-us/news-and-blogs/a-beginners-guide-to-completing-couch-to-5k/)
- Runna's Plan Realignment prompt appears when the runner has missed more than three workouts or a full week. It offers three choices: skip last week's missed workouts; move them later in the week without doubling up; or continue the plan unchanged (skipping the missed weeks). The choice cannot be undone once confirmed, and the prompt only appears if missed workouts were left unticked rather than marked skipped. — [Runna support, plan realignment](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/10026375-how-to-use-the-plan-realignment-feature)
- Runna's rule of thumb: skip a run you have decided not to do; rearrange one you still plan to complete. A skipped run is removed and the plan adjusts around the remaining sessions. — [Runna support, skipping and missed sessions](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/15012850-how-and-when-to-skip-a-run-managing-missed-sessions-in-your-training-plan)
- On Runna plans with a race or goal end date, workouts can be dragged within the same week or into the adjacent week; on plans without a date, only within current or future weeks, and the calendar re-spaces later sessions itself. — [Runna support, training calendar](https://intercom.help/runna/en/articles/10137793-how-to-use-and-manage-your-training-calendar)
- Runna added a "Not feeling 100%" tool for adapting a plan around illness. — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/wellness/fitness/im-deep-into-marathon-training-fighting-the-flu-and-runnas-new-not-feeling-100-percent-tool-couldnt-be-more-timely); [Runna support, feeling unwell](https://intercom.help/runna/en/articles/12809806-feeling-unwell-how-to-adapt-your-training-plan)
- Strava's own running training plans are moving to Runna; Strava's built-in weekly surface is the Training Log, "weekly overviews of your recent activities", which is a record of the past, not a plan. A 2015 Strava post conceded the product fell short on planning future training. — [Strava support, goals and training plans](https://support.strava.com/en-us/collections/19668882-goals-and-training-plans); [Strava support, Training Log](https://support.strava.com/hc/articles/206535704-Training-Log); [Strava on Medium, 2015](https://medium.com/on-strava/objective-8399e5163ca8)
- Nike Run Club offers plans by race distance (5K, 10K, half, marathon; an eight-week 5K plan; a 4-week "Get Started" plan). Nike's own advice: "every training plan is written in pencil". — [Nike help, NRC plans](https://www.nike.com/help/a/nrc-plan); [Nike, "How to deal with running setbacks"](https://www.nike.com/a/how-to-deal-with-running-setbacks)
- Reported NRC weaknesses (anecdotal): users could not go back to a previous week and add a workout; a run not recognised by the plan left users unsure whether the plan adapts. — [App Store reviews, NRC](https://apps.apple.com/nz/app/nike-run-club-running-fitness/id387771637); [HealthUnlocked thread](https://healthunlocked.com/marathon-running/posts/141197116/nike-run-club-half-marathon-training-plan)
- Runkeeper's advice for a missed run: "it's best to skip it and continue with your plan as written". — [Runkeeper](https://runkeeper.com/cms/training/what-to-do-when-you-miss-a-run-while-training-for-a-race/)
- Peloton programs: a fixed number of weeks and classes per week stated up front; later classes unlock only in order; difficulty rises over the weeks. — [Peloton blog](https://www.onepeloton.com/blog/peloton-programs)

### Inferences
- The unit that plans are shown in is the week, with "this week" as the default view and the whole plan one level up. A semester is the degree's week: a container of three to six items with a start and an end.
- Runna's realignment prompt is a decision surfaced only when it is needed, as one question with three plain answers and a stated consequence. It does not show the runner a re-solved calendar and ask them to interpret it. That is the same shape as "this plan needs an approval: here are your choices".
- Runna separates two verbs the user might conflate (skip versus move). Naming the two outcomes prevents the plan from silently filling with overdue items.
- The NRC complaints show what happens when a plan does not say whether it noticed a deviation: the user stops trusting it. A plan must visibly acknowledge "you are behind / this changed" even if it changes nothing.
- A date-bound plan (race day) and an open-ended plan behave differently in Runna: with a fixed end date, missed work is dropped or compressed; without one, the plan slides. A degree with a target graduation term is the date-bound kind.

### Gaps
- No source found that states what a training-plan home screen shows first (I believe, unsourced, that Runna and NRC open on the current week with today's or the next session at the top and a "week N of M" label, and that Couch to 5K shows a grid of weeks with three run ticks each). Verify in the apps.
- No published retention or completion data comparing plan presentations.
- Couch to 5K's guidance on repeating a week when it felt too hard is widely repeated but I found no NHS page stating it in this session.

## 5. Game-style maps and skill trees: what they do well for "what's next", where they fail

### Takeaway
A saga map answers "what's next" with zero decisions: one line, one next level, a position that can be compared with a friend's ("what level are you on?"). Designers themselves say it only works when being stuck is tolerable and the content is a sequence; skill trees give choice but are criticised for paralysis when options are many, for fake choice when a branch is really a rail, and for prerequisites that force unwanted picks.

### Cited Findings
- The "saga-map" model is "the linear map of levels in games like 'Candy Crush Saga' that players progress through one-by-one". It "succeeds only when paired with a gameplay system that convinces players to happily accept the state of being stuck"; skill-based genres such as bubble shooters "fail to motivate players to continue playing a level after repeated losses, and thus are a poor fit". — [Game Developer, "Rethinking progression in mobile puzzle games"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/rethinking-progression-in-mobile-puzzle-games)
- King co-founder Sebastian Knutsson on why the map worked: "It worked as a single-player journey ... Players wanted to beat the next level, they felt good about their progress, and they asked others, 'What level are you on?'" (Search excerpt; attributed to an Apple App Store "Behind the Scenes" story in the results, outlet not confirmed by a full read.) — [App Store story](https://apps.apple.com/us/story/id1462263204)
- Map formats compared: a traditional terrain world map suits games with few or no level updates and gives a poor mobile experience; an island-style map with separate landmarks lets developers "change and add land marks/levels with great flexibility" and suits frequently updated games. — [Game Developer, "Mobile game maps: which format is right for you"](https://gamedeveloper.com/production/mobile-game-maps-which-format-is-right-for-you)
- Hub worlds give "a non-linear feel" and "a small amount of strategic decision making as to which levels to beat first", but heavy gating of visit order makes a hub feel linear anyway. (Forum discussion.) — [GameDev.net thread on hub worlds](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/655978-hub-worlds-and-their-use/5150427/)
- Skill-tree choice design (Lars Doucet): "Choices should consist of limited options. If there's too many to pick from, choice paralysis sets in." Options must be clear: "If the player doesn't know what each one will do, the choice isn't interesting, and therefore isn't fun." — [Game Developer, "Upgrades, equipment, and skill trees"](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/upgrades-equipment-and-skill-trees)
- Simulated choice: in many trees the only real decision is the first point, after which the player is "more or less on a railroad track" down the branch. (Forum post, opinion.) — [Blizzard forums, "On the significance of skill trees in gaming"](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/d4/t/on-the-significance-of-skill-trees-in-gaming/145628)
- Prerequisites: they should make thematic sense, and late-tree locks can force players to take skills they do not want. (Forum post, opinion.) — [GOG forum, "Skill trees are awful, give me skill tables instead"](https://gog.com/forum/general/skill_trees_are_awful_give_me_skill_tables_instead)
- Saga-map difficulty is not monotonic: later levels are generally harder but with plateaus and easier stretches where progress is rapid. (Secondary source, author unverified.) — [Gamerboom archive](https://gamerboom.com/archives/96972)

### Inferences
- A saga map shows exactly one number that matters (your level) and one next action (the next node). Its social power comes from that single comparable number.
- The saga map's precondition, a true sequence, is where a degree differs: a degree is a set of buckets with choices inside, some prerequisites, and two programs that can share courses. A literal map of courses would either lie about order or become a skill tree.
- The island-style map (separate landmarks, each with its own levels) is the map form designers pick when content is grouped and changes often. Requirement blocks ("major", "general education", "electives") resemble islands more than a single road; semesters resemble a road.
- The skill-tree critiques map onto degree planning directly: many eligible courses for one slot is choice paralysis; a requirement whose description does not say what counts is an unclear option; a prerequisite chain that dictates the next four semesters is a rail presented as a choice. Doucet's rule (few options, each one's effect clear) is the test for any "pick a course" step.
- Duolingo's petitioners and the forum critics want the same thing from opposite sides: honest choice. A line that hides real choices angers experts; a tree that shows fake choices annoys everyone.

### Gaps
- The Game Maker's Toolkit video "Building Better Skill Trees" surfaced only as a subtitle file; its arguments were not extracted.
- No rigorous (peer-reviewed or published-data) study of saga maps versus hub maps for retention was found; the evidence is developer essays and forum opinion.
- No source on how Candy Crush draws locked, current and completed levels (unsourced: completed levels show stars earned, the current one carries the player's avatar, later ones are greyed, and episode gates pause progress).

## 6. One-question-per-screen guided flows (GOV.UK, Typeform, Noom, Lemonade, TurboTax): why they work, completion evidence

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence is qualitative and from the UK Government Digital Service, which after repeated user research recommends starting every form with one thing per page and merging pages only when research supports it. Numerical claims come mostly from form vendors and conflict with each other; long consumer quizzes (Noom, 70 to 113 screens) work through progressive commitment in subscription funnels, and practitioners warn that a chat-shaped form with no way back frustrates people with complicated situations.

### Cited Findings
- GOV.UK guidance: start by splitting a form across multiple pages "with each page containing just one thing", such as one piece of information, one decision or one question; "user research will tell you when you can merge pages together". — [GOV.UK Service Manual, form structure](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/form-structure); [GOV.UK Design System, question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages)
- GDS researched "one thing per page" "in great depth" on Register to Vote and GOV.UK Verify (Tim Paul, 2015), and advises teams to start with it and see how it goes with their own users. It is "not about short pages, but about one thing per page". — [GDS Design Notes, "One thing per page"](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2015/07/03/one-thing-per-page/)
- GDS user research: "very simple one-question-per-page designs work better for our users than anything more complicated - at least as a starting point". The team stopped recommending accordion forms. In a Verify test, splitting username and password onto separate pages changed the author's view. Removing progress indicators from a long form "made no difference at all to the completion rates". — [GDS User Research blog, "No more accordions: how to choose a form structure"](https://userresearch.blog.gov.uk/2015/08/13/no-more-accordions-how-to-choose-a-form-structure/)
- GDS on why it works: on a phone users find it simpler to move through one question per page than several; error handling is much easier per page; the team did not observe users reacting badly to multiple pages or page loads. — [Inside GOV.UK, improving content display on mobile](https://insidegovuk.blog.gov.uk/2015/06/26/improving-content-display-mobile/); [GDS Design Notes](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2015/07/03/one-thing-per-page/)
- Vendor numbers disagree: Typeform cites an average completion rate of 57% on one help page and 47% on another; Jotform claims completion up to 36% higher with one question at a time, with no visible source. — [Typeform help](https://help.typeform.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029615911); [Typeform help (alternate)](https://typeform.com/help/whats-the-average-completion-rate-of-a-typeform); [Jotform blog](https://www.jotform.com/blog/one-at-a-time-question-on-survey-form/)
- Counter-evidence: Paperform's 2020 analysis of its own forms found single-page forms had the highest median completion rate, 82.01%. It compares single-page with multi-page, not one-question-per-screen specifically, and does not control for form length. — [Paperform forms study 2020](https://paperform.co/forms-data-analysis)
- Caveats from form vendors: one-at-a-time layouts can lengthen total completion time, and work best for short or medium-length questionnaires. — [Fillout, one-question-at-a-time vs single-page](https://fillout.com/blog/one-question-at-a-time-form); [Jotform](https://www.jotform.com/blog/one-at-a-time-question-on-survey-form/)
- Noom and Flo run onboarding quizzes of 70 to 113 screens. The argued mechanism is progressive commitment: the more effort put in, the harder it is to walk away at the paywall. The same analysis warns a long quiz can hurt a product whose value is visible immediately. — [FirstApp, "Quiz onboarding: mechanics and limits"](https://www.firstapp.fr/en/blog/quiz-onboarding-pourquoi-ca-marche-et-quand-eviter)
- A progress bar is credited with a 10 to 20% rise in completion in Noom-style funnels (a marketing blog citing Funnelfox; the search excerpt did not say which of the two listed pages carried it; unverified). — [FirstApp](https://www.firstapp.fr/en/blog/quiz-onboarding-pourquoi-ca-marche-et-quand-eviter); [Botsi, 13 onboarding principles](https://www.botsi.com/blog/13-onboarding-principles-high-converting-apps)
- Lemonade's "Maya" asks one question at a time in a chat interface, each answer triggering the next; described as feeling "more like a guided quiz than a form"; a vendor case study says quotes come in 90 seconds. No Lemonade-published conversion figure was found. — [Twofold funnel research page](https://funnel-researcher-dashboard.internal.trytwofold.com/screenshots/lemonade); [Heyflow](https://heyflow.com/blog/how-lemonade-got-more-leads-through-6-tricks/); [Perspective AI case study (vendor)](https://getperspective.ai/blog/lemonade-case-study-conversational-ai-insurance)
- Warnings: conversational intake works for simple quotes but can frustrate people with complex situations, with "no easy way to go back and change answers"; and a "chat-shaped form" keeps the annoyance of a 40-field form without the benefit. — [Twofold funnel research page](https://funnel-researcher-dashboard.internal.trytwofold.com/screenshots/lemonade); [Perspective AI, Hims & Hers (vendor)](https://getperspective.ai/blog/hims-hers-ai-patient-intake-5b-telehealth-replaced-forms)
- Duolingo's own onboarding evidence for a forced single choice: making users pick a streak goal instead of accepting a default raised the goals chosen and later retention, with no drop-off from the added step. — [Ali Abouelatta thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1594707015032455168)

### Inferences
- The credible claim is narrower than the marketing one: one thing per screen reduces errors and confusion, especially on a phone, and is the safe starting structure. It is not proven to raise completion in general, and Paperform's data points the other way for short forms.
- GDS's finding that a progress indicator made no difference to completion sits against the consumer-funnel belief that a progress bar helps. The two are measuring different things (a required government task versus an optional sign-up), so neither generalises cleanly.
- The stated failure mode of conversational flows, no way back and poor fit for complicated cases, is the one that would hurt a student with two degrees and petitions. A guided flow needs visible, editable answers.
- A sequence of single questions is a natural fit for the "advisor questions" part of a degree plan (each is a yes / no with a stated consequence), and Runna's realignment prompt (section 4) is the same pattern in a plan context.

### Gaps
- TurboTax: no source found in this session on its interview design or any completion data. (Unsourced: TurboTax is the best-known "interview" pattern, asking one topic at a time with a running refund figure at the top.)
- No independent controlled study isolating one-question-per-screen as the cause of higher completion was found.
- Lemonade's first-party design writing on Maya was not found; all Lemonade findings are third-party.

## 7. For each pattern: what the user sees first, how many numbers are on screen, how the next action is shown

### Takeaway
Across every pattern the first thing seen is a position or a state, not a table: where you are on a line, whether a shape is closed, which week this is. Numbers on the first screen are few (zero to about three), and the next action is a single prominent control attached to the current item.

### Cited Findings
- Duolingo: the home screen is "a path that you'll follow step by step"; sections and units are one tap away behind the section label, and closing that list returns "to your current place". — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/); [Duolingo blog, review lessons](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-to-review-lessons-on-duolingo)
- Apple: "Three rings ... One goal: Close them every day"; the design was built "around a visual" in deliberate contrast to trackers that lead with numbers. — [Apple](https://www.apple.com/om/watch/close-your-rings/); [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/content/jay-blahnik-what-separates-apple-watch-other-fitness-trackers)
- Khan Academy: one headline figure (course mastery percent, or in the newer experience a count of skills at Proficient within the unit), with per-skill state as one of four named levels. — [Khan Academy help](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/48411237993357-How-is-my-mastery-percentage-calculated); [Khan Academy update](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/48431088309645-Update-How-mastery-percentage-is-calculated-on-Khan-Academy)
- Headspace: an ascending timeline "with your current pack at the top". — [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.in/I've-used-a-meditation-app-loved-by-Wall-Streeters-and-Olympic-athletes-daily-for-the-past-8-months-and-now-I-can't-imagine-my-life-without-it/Your-packs-are-arranged-in-an-ascending-timeline-with-your-current-pack-at-the-top-/slideshow/53802288.cms)
- Training plans: the whole plan is stated as two numbers (Couch to 5K "9 weeks ... 3 times a week"; Peloton "5 weeks ... 3 classes per week"), and deviation is handled by a prompt with three choices (Runna). — [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/couch-to-5k-running-plan); [Peloton](https://www.onepeloton.com/blog/peloton-programs); [Runna support](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/10026375-how-to-use-the-plan-realignment-feature)
- Saga map: one line of levels taken "one-by-one"; the single shared number is the level ("What level are you on?"). — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/rethinking-progression-in-mobile-puzzle-games); [App Store story](https://apps.apple.com/us/story/id1462263204)
- Guided flows: each page holds "just one thing", one piece of information, one decision or one question. — [GOV.UK Service Manual](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/form-structure)

### Inferences

**Pattern by pattern (my reading; the cited sources above anchor the structure, while the counts of numbers on screen are my judgment from general knowledge of these apps and should be checked against the apps themselves):**

| Pattern | Seen first | Numbers on the first screen | How the next action is shown | Where it breaks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Duolingo path | Your position on one line | About three, all in a thin top bar (streak, gems, hearts); none on the path itself | One raised node with a start callout | Loss of choice; position jumps when content is rearranged |
| Apple rings | Three shapes, closed or open | Zero on the rings; three one tap in | None on the shape; the gap in the ring is the prompt | Daily reset does not suit long goals; bars read more accurately; two ring sets on one screen confuse |
| Khan mastery | One percent or one count, then a list of units | One headline, plus small per-unit counts | The next unstarted or lowest-level skill | The headline moves slowly on long courses; inconsistent numbers across screens erode trust (Brilliant) |
| Headspace / Brilliant / Peloton | An ordered short list, current item on top | One or two ("day 4 of 10", "week 2 of 5") | A play or continue button on the current item | Strict sequence gating frustrates people who want to skip |
| Training plan | This week's sessions | Two or three (week N of M, sessions this week, end date) | Today's or the next session | Silent about deviations unless designed for it; Runna's prompt is the fix |
| Saga map | Your level on a road | One (level) | The next node | Only fits a true sequence |
| Skill tree | The whole graph | Many (points, costs) | Any unlocked node, chosen by the user | Paralysis, fake choice, forced prerequisites |
| One question per screen | A single question | Zero or one (step count, optional) | One primary button | No overview; hard to go back; poor for complicated cases |

**Implications for a degree-progress screen (evidence-led observations, not a design):**

- **Lead with position, not inventory.** The patterns that feel simple open on "where am I and what is next" (a node, a week, a ring gap). The current degree screen opens on an inventory (rings, bars, requirement cards, table). Duolingo's stated reason for the path, learners unsure whether they were using it the "correct" or "best" way, is the same doubt a student has in front of ten open requirements.
- **One number as the headline, said the same way everywhere.** Khan uses one percent with a strict counting rule; the saga map uses one level; Brilliant was criticised when the same progress read differently in different places. A screen that carries Penn's percent, an estimate with plan, a by-target figure and a best-case figure is four headlines.
- **A ring is a "closed or not" mark, and two ring sets side by side is a pattern Apple warns against.** Apple's own rules: do not mix ring styles, do not use one ring set for two subjects, keep the meaning of each ring fixed. For two degrees, the evidence favours either one shape per degree with a fixed meaning or a non-ring encoding; and the bars-versus-rings finding says a bar is the more accurately read form when the question is "how much is left".
- **The semester is the natural "week".** Training plans show a long goal as a row of small containers with the current one open, state the whole plan in two numbers ("9 weeks, 3 runs a week"), and show an end date. "N semesters, finishing spring 2028" is the degree's equivalent sentence.
- **Surface a problem as one question with two or three plain answers and a stated consequence.** Runna's realignment prompt and GOV.UK's one-thing-per-page both point the same way for approvals and advisor questions: one at a time, each with what a yes changes, answers visible and editable afterwards (the documented failure of chat-style flows is no way back).
- **Keep an honest door to the full picture.** Duolingo's backlash and the skill-tree critiques both come from choice being hidden or faked. Duolingo kept a section list and a jump-ahead test behind one tap. A simple first screen does not remove the need for "show me everything" for the expert user; it moves it one level down.
- **Say when the ground moved.** Duolingo users felt "stranded" when a course update shifted their position; NRC users lost trust when the plan did not say whether it noticed a missed run. A degree position that changes because Penn's audit was re-read, or because a what-if replaced the declared program, needs a visible "this changed because" line.
- **A true line only fits a true sequence.** The saga map's own designers say it depends on the content being a sequence. A degree is partly ordered (semesters, prerequisite chains, senior project halves) and partly a set of buckets with choices. The evidence supports a line for time (semesters) and something bucket-like (islands, short ladders of named states) for requirements, and warns against drawing courses as a single road.
- **Name states with words.** Khan (Familiar, Proficient, Mastered), Runna (skip versus move) and Peloton (week 2 of 5) all make the state speakable. "Done, in progress, planned, needs approval" is that kind of vocabulary; colour alone is not.
- **Daily-habit mechanics do not transfer.** Rings and streaks get their pull from a daily reset. A degree moves a few times a semester. The transferable parts of Duolingo are the single next step and the celebration of a finished unit, not the streak.
