# Degree audit and planning tools: how they present progress, what confuses students, what the better ones do

Research date: 2026-11-27. Method note for the report writer: this cloud session could
search the web but could fetch full pages from `github.com` only (every other host was
refused by the session's network allowlist: `law.utexas.edu`, `www.idsnews.com`,
`www.college.upenn.edu`, `advising.cis.upenn.edu`, `thedp.com`, `jamnavarro.github.io`).
So most findings below come from search-result extracts of the cited pages, not from a
full read. Quoted phrases are as the search extracts returned them; check a quote against
its page before printing it as verbatim. Where a claim rests on a vendor's own marketing,
it says so.

## 1. Ellucian Degree Works: layout, What-If, Plans, and documented complaints

### Takeaway
Degree Works is a long vertical "worksheet" of requirement blocks with status icons, a
"Still Needed" line per unmet rule, and two top-of-page progress figures (requirements %
and credits %) that often disagree; its best-documented failure points are the leftover
"Fallthrough" / "Not Counted" bins, a silent "best fit" engine that moves courses between
requirements, What-If results that are not saved or shared, and a default view that
several schools say cannot show a double major or dual degree correctly.

### Cited Findings
- The audit is described by schools as an online version of a requirements check sheet; at Drexel it has two parts, "The Worksheet (also referred to as audit)" and "The Plan". — [Drexel Degree Works guide](https://drexel.edu/~/media/Files/drexelCentral/pdfs/degreeworks.ashx?la=en)
- Unfulfilled requirements are labelled "Still Needed" and marked with an empty red box (South Alabama's student tips); Texas Tech's guide lists a separate condensed "Still Needed View" showing only what remains. — [South Alabama student tips](https://aesta.southalabama.edu/departments/registrar/resources/degreeworks/03-student-tips.pdf); [Texas Tech audit guide](https://www.depts.ttu.edu/degreeworks/Training/DWAuditGuide.pdf)
- In the newer "Responsive Dashboard" the two progress bars became two wheels under the student header (requirements % and credits %, plus GPA); requirement icons changed from squares to circles; each block header carries a Complete (green) / In-Progress / Incomplete (red) label; in-progress courses are shown in blue or with "REG" in the grade column. Schools state outright that "the Requirements progress will not always equal the Credits progress." — [Farmingdale Responsive Dashboard student guide](https://www.farmingdale.edu/registrar/degree-works/pdf/fsc-degreeworks-student-manual.pdf); [Rowan training manual](https://sites.rowan.edu/registrar/_docs/degreeworks_responsive-dashboard_manualfinal.pdf); [Xavier info sheet](https://www.xavier.edu/registrar/documents/xavier-degreeworks-update-infosheet.pdf); [WCSU user guide](https://www.wcsu.edu/registrar/wp-content/uploads/sites/99/2023/02/Degree-Works-User-Guide-2.21.23.pdf)
- The student can choose whether in-progress and preregistered classes are included when an audit is processed; "Look Ahead" and "What-If" were merged in the Responsive Dashboard (one school renamed look-ahead "Future Classes"). — [UTRGV faculty/staff guide](https://www.utrgv.edu/ucentral/_files/documents/degreeworks-files/degreeworks-responsive-dashboard-faculty-staff-guide-final.pdf); [WVNET sneak peek](https://tlc.wvnet.edu/wp-content/courses/DegreeWorks%20Responsive%20Dashboard/A%20Sneak%20Peek%20at%20the%20Responsive%20Dashboard%20&%20Web%20transit.pdf)
- "Fallthrough" holds every course not used for a specific requirement (still counted in GPA and hours). Appalachian State tells advisors not to ignore it because a course there may need a substitution; Charlotte says a Fallthrough course that should apply "will require an academic petition". — [Oklahoma State advisor FAQ](https://registrar.okstate.edu/degree-works/degree-works-advisor-faqs); [Appalachian State FAQ](https://faa.appstate.edu/node?page=8); [Charlotte FAQ](https://ninercentral.charlotte.edu/?p=2627)
- A separate "Not Counted" block takes courses over a requirement's limit (Syracuse) or at the wrong level (UAA: all 600-level courses for undergraduates). — [Syracuse student FAQ](https://dw.syr.edu/faqs/student-faqs); [UAA Degree Works help](https://www.uaa.alaska.edu/students/progress/degreeworks-help.cshtml)
- The engine is "best fit": "classes may apply to different sections as you take more courses" (Georgia). So a course a student saw under one requirement can move later with no notice. — [UGA Degree Works FAQ](https://reg.uga.edu/general-information/faqs/degreeworks.html)
- What-If shows "how your completed or in-progress courses would specifically apply, and what requirements or courses would still be needed" for another program. Limits: Wake Forest and FGCU say What-If scenarios are not stored, so an advisor sees one only if it is run together or printed; Louisiana keeps the last three in a drop-down. — [Wake Forest FAQ](https://registrar.wfu.edu/?p=923); [FGCU Degree Works](https://www.fgcu.edu/recordsandregistration/degree-works); [Louisiana guide](https://institutionaleffectiveness.louisiana.edu/node/69)
- Every school page carries the same disclaimer pattern: the audit "is not your academic transcript nor is it an official notification of completion of degree requirements"; disputes go to the advisor, who routes corrections. — [PennWest Degree Works](https://my.pennwest.edu/registrar/degree-works); [Wilmington FAQ](https://calendar.wilmu.edu/mywilmu/registration-degree-works-faq.aspx); [UAA help](https://www.uaa.alaska.edu/students/progress/degreeworks-help.cshtml)
- Documented complaint, student government: the University of South Carolina's 112th Student Senate passed a resolution (SBL 112-84) calling for more accurate DegreeWorks audits, saying the current state "may prove confusing and frustrating for students". — [USC Student Senate SBL 112-84](https://sc.edu/about/offices_and_divisions/advising/documents/sbl_112_84.pdf)
- Documented complaint, research: a Texas State thesis ("Design and Construction of Visual Degree Audit") surveyed 194 students on the Degree Works audit and concluded students "still have difficulty interpreting and utilizing" it, and prototyped a visual replacement tested with five students. — [Texas State thesis](https://digital.library.txst.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/54896e48-fa83-4ab1-9f69-1b01713c5d11/content)
- A consultant's list of "5 common issues with Ellucian Degree Works audits" exists (transfer credit, exceptions not entered, etc.); it is a vendor-adjacent blog, not research. — [David Kent Consulting](https://davidkentconsulting.com/blog/common-issues-with-degree-works-audits-explained/)
- A community project, "flow-works", turns a Degree Works audit into an animated prerequisite flowchart "of your path to graduation" in the browser: evidence that students want a path view the audit does not give. — [frostdev-ops/flow-works](https://github.com/frostdev-ops/flow-works)

### Inferences
- Degree Works answers "which rules are unmet" and not "when do I finish" or "what do I take next"; the number of schools that publish multi-page guides just to read the audit is itself evidence that the layout is not self-explanatory.
- Two headline percentages that disagree (requirements vs credits) need a sentence of explanation on nearly every school's guide. A single headline figure, or one figure with the other as a plain count, avoids that explanation.
- The leftover bins (Fallthrough, Not Counted, Insufficient) are where trust is lost: they are named in system language, sit at the bottom, and the only stated remedy is "ask your advisor / file a petition".
- "Best fit" reshuffling is correct behaviour that looks like a bug, because nothing tells the student a course moved or why.

### Gaps
- No Reddit thread or student-newspaper opinion piece specifically criticising Degree Works surfaced through search (school FAQs dominate results). The complaint evidence here is one student-senate resolution, one thesis and the FAQ patterns. Reddit was not directly searchable in this session.
- The Texas State thesis's survey percentages were not retrievable (PDF host not fetchable); only its framing is cited.
- Ellucian's own documentation of the "Plans" (Student Educational Planner) screens was not read; the Plan is known here only as "the second part" beside the Worksheet.

## 2. Stellic: audit view, planner, pathways, progress display, and reception after adoption

### Takeaway
Stellic's model is one profile with a "Progress" tab (the audit) and an "audit-aware" planner where dragging a course into a term immediately shows which requirements it would fill; it distinguishes an Official audit (taken + registered) from a Planned audit (plus planned courses) with a small icon vocabulary, and reception is "largely positive" for new students but shows a real learning curve for upperclassmen moved off a familiar tool.

### Cited Findings
- Origin: built by Carnegie Mellon students with their advisors; CMU's 2018 story covers the pilot, and Stellic's own page claims 80% student adoption at CMU (vendor-sourced). — [CMU news, 2018](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2018/april/course-planning-platform.html); [Stellic CMU success story](https://www.stellic.com/success-stories/carnegie-mellon)
- Audit placement: "Stellic displays a student's official degree audit in the Progress tab of the student profile"; courses are also grouped by term with credits attempted/earned and term GPA. — [Columbia SIS help: Stellic resources](https://sishelp.sis.columbia.edu/content/stellic-degree-audit-resources-arts-and-sciences-departments)
- Two audits, one toggle: "the Official audit (which only shows classes already taken or registered) or the Planned audit (which shows classes planned out within Stellic, and courses already taken or registered)". Johns Hopkins states the same split and that adding a course in Stellic "is for planning purposes only". — [Guilford College: Using Stellic](https://sites.google.com/guilford.edu/guilfordcollege-usingstellic/home); [JHU EP degree audit](https://ep.jhu.edu/new-students/degree-audit/)
- Icon vocabulary (UW-Milwaukee's configuration): green checkmark = complete (fulfilled or waived); orange clock = in progress; orange single checkmark = planned course or requirement with a planned course; red box = remaining; grey dash = optional. A right-side icon marks "Course is counting toward multiple requirements, hover for details", another marks a course "used as an equivalent to the required course". Case Western uses yellow double checkmarks (being taken) and a yellow single checkmark (planned). — [UWM audit icons](https://uwm.edu/registrar/enrollment/stellic/audit-icons/); [UWM student guide PDF](https://uwm.edu/registrar/wp-content/uploads/sites/671/2026/03/Audit-Icons-3.2.26.pdf); [Case Western audit icons](https://case.edu/registrar/sites/default/files/2024-02/Stellic%20-%20Audit%20Icons.pdf)
- Planner: CMU says the planner "allows a student to drag and drop courses from the course search into their degree plan, checking for pre-requisites first". JHU's pages say that when a course is planned the system shows which requirements it would satisfy and suggests courses that knock out the most open requirements, to "understand the most efficient path to graduation". — [CMU Stellic page](https://www.cmu.edu/es/stellic/index.html); [JHU Stellic student resources](https://info.stellic.jhu.edu/student-resources/)
- Pathways: Columbia Engineering defines them as "recommended degree sequences as reflected in the Columbia Engineering Bulletin" that a student can load into the plan; IU publishes its pathways both inside Stellic and publicly. — [Columbia Engineering: Stellic](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/academics/academic-resources/stellic); [IU Student Success: Stellic](https://studentsuccess.iu.edu/stellic/index.html)
- What-if: a what-if audit "works like the official audit, except that you pick the courses and the programs separately"; Stellic's API docs warn that exceptions that do not fit the rebuilt requirements "can be marked removed" and say not to run what-ifs for students with exceptions. — [Stellic partner API: what-if audit](https://docs.stellic.com/stellic-partner-api/student/run-a-what-if-audit-for-a-student)
- Unmatched courses: Stellic audits have an "Unmatched" section; FSU's FAQ describes unmatched courses as ones that "may not be counting for specific degree requirements", and Trinity's advisor training tells advisors to "review the Unmatched section" when a planned course could count but does not. — [FSU Stellic FAQ](https://stellic.fsu.edu/students/stellic-faq); [Trinity College adviser training](https://www.trincoll.edu/registrar/wp-content/uploads/sites/120/2025/09/Trinity-College_Stellic-Adviser-User-Training.pdf)
- Reception, UVA: the Cavalier Daily headline is "Students express contentment with new Stellic advising program" (March 2025); the program director says "feedback has been largely positive" while input is still being gathered. UVA keeps registration in SIS: Stellic "is for planning purposes only". — [Cavalier Daily, 2025-03](https://www.cavalierdaily.com/article/2025/03/students-express-contentment-with-new-stellic-advising-program); [UVA Stellic student features](https://stellic.virginia.edu/student-features)
- Reception, Indiana: Stellic replaces the Academic Advising Report and iGPS. A senior told the IDS, "I definitely prefer iGPS just because it's easier to navigate compared to Stellic"; freshmen found the switch easy because they had no iGPS habit; an advisor preferred iGPS's course search. By October 2026 students are required to use it, peer coaches see more upperclassmen seeking help, and one coach says "there's definitely a learning curve, as with any new system". — [Indiana Daily Student, 2026-03](https://www.idsnews.com/article/2026/03/iu-students-campus-stellic-schedule-classes-course-igps-academic-advisor); [Indiana Daily Student, 2026-10](https://www.idsnews.com/article/2026/10/iu-stellic-class-scheduling-advising-how-to)
- Reception, institutions (vendor-sourced unless noted): UChicago's registrar: "We have received such great feedback from both the advisors and the students"; UW-Stout's registrar praises that student plans "are based on those exact requirements". Utah awarded Stellic its contract for "degree audit, 4-year planning, and registration" in 2026 (university source). — [Stellic: UChicago story](https://stellic.com/customer-story/the-university-of-chicago); [Stellic: UW-Stout story](https://stellic.com/customer-story/university-of-wisconsin-stout); [Utah degree audit modernization](https://degreeaudit.utah.edu/degree-audit-modernization/)
- Stellic's own advice to advising offices: "Why isn't this counting?" tickets trace to a short list of causes (transfer credit, described as the most common reason an audit looks incomplete; approvals that live "only in an email or a hallway conversation"; double-counting rules the student cannot see; grade/constraint problems), and a self-explaining audit would prevent many from being filed. Marketing material, but it names the question students actually send. — [Stellic: cut "why isn't this counting" tickets](https://www.stellic.com/resources/reduce-degree-audit-support-tickets); [Stellic: audit says I'm missing requirements](https://www.stellic.com/resources/degree-audit-missing-requirements)

### Inferences
- The idea worth noticing is the Official / Planned toggle over the same requirement list: one view of truth, one view of intent, same layout. It makes "planned" a state of a requirement, not a separate screen.
- "Show what this course would fill before I add it" is the planner feature both CMU and JHU lead with. It answers "does this count" at the moment of choice.
- The IU reaction says familiarity beats design for existing users: a redesign that moves things will be judged worse by people fluent in the old layout, and better by newcomers.
- Even Stellic needs a published icon legend per campus, and its meanings differ between campuses (orange vs yellow, clock vs double check). A small icon set still needs words beside it.

### Gaps
- No independent survey or usability study of Stellic was found; student-paper coverage found is two campuses (UVA, IU), and the UVA article's student quotes were not retrievable.
- How Stellic chooses between candidate placements for a course ("most efficient path" ranking) is not documented publicly.
- Whether Stellic has a native mobile app or a tuned phone layout could not be confirmed (see question 8).

## 3. Other vendors: distinctive presentation ideas only

### Takeaway
The distinctive ideas are few: uAchieve's clickable category charts over a text audit with three-colour status, Workday's single "progress wheel" (which UBC removed for being inaccurate), and audits that separate satisfied / in progress / not satisfied per requirement; none of the vendor material found shows a path or a graduation date as the lead element.

### Cited Findings
- uAchieve (CollegeSource): an "Hours" pie chart of total hours toward the degree (Illinois) and an interactive bar chart with one bar per category: University Requirements, GPA, General Education, College, Total Units, Major, Electives (UC Davis); bars click through to requirements and sub-requirements (Lehigh). — [Illinois uAchieve documentation](https://www.registrar.illinois.edu/faculty-staff/reporting-and-data-access/degree-audit-reporting-system/u-achieve-selfservice-documentation-for-advisors/); [UC Davis: navigating the audit](https://registrar.ucdavis.edu/faculty-staff/uachieve-advisors/accessing-degree-audit/navigating-degree-audit); [Lehigh audit guide](https://ras.lehigh.edu/content/degree-audit-instructions)
- uAchieve colour convention at Miami (Ohio): completed green, in progress blue, still needed orange; "In progress (IP)" explicitly covers both currently enrolled and registered for an upcoming term. — [Miami University degree audit report](https://miamioh.edu/onestop/academic-records/degree-audit-report.html)
- uAchieve chart limits: Lehigh says some graphs are "not utilized because of program complexity"; Ramapo notes graphs and charts do not print. — [Lehigh audit guide](https://ras.lehigh.edu/content/degree-audit-instructions); [Ramapo FAQ](https://www.ramapo.edu/studentsuccess/wp-content/uploads/sites/61/2018/11/DEGREE-AUDIT-FREQUENTLY-ASKED-QUESTIONS.pdf)
- Workday Student "Academic Progress": shows which requirements are satisfied, in progress, and unsatisfied; a requirement is not marked satisfied until all its courses are complete, so in-progress courses read as still needed (Palm Beach State help desk lists this as a daily confusion). — [Palm Beach State KB](https://help.palmbeachstate.edu/TDClient/1971/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=102717)
- Workday at UBC: the university removed the "academic progress wheel" for some programs, including Arts (January 2026), because it "did not reflect completion percentages accurately". The AMS VP Academic said inaccuracies in the underlying progress reports were "a major source of student anxiety". The Ubyssey reports 78% of students had found the old portal / Degree Navigator useful and only 24% felt the Workday version met their needs. Students must now read the full report to know what remains. — [The Ubyssey](https://ubyssey.ca/news/workday-academic-progress-wheel/); [UBC Arts: Academic Progress Report](https://www.arts.ubc.ca/degree-planning/year-by-year-planning/academic-progress-report/)
- Workday at Rochester (UR Student): about 175 students were sent another person's degree-completion information after the switch to the new audit system. A data-handling failure, not a presentation one, but it shows how sensitive students are to audit errors. — [Campus Times, 2020-12](https://www.campustimes.org/2020/12/12/students-sent-wrong-information-in-degree-audit-mix-up/)
- Coursedog / AACRAO white paper: a survey of 653 registrar-community respondents found trust in degree-audit accuracy varies by role, with unit degree auditors trusting the tool more than advisors or students. — [Coursedog / AACRAO white paper](https://coursedog.com/whitepaper/aacrao-use-of-degree-audit-and-education-planning-solutions)
- Penn State's 2010s rollout paired a new audit with a planner so students and advisers can "track degrees and plan path [to] graduation" (institutional announcement). — [Penn State news](https://www.psu.edu/news/administration/story/new-tools-help-students-advisers-track-degrees-and-plan-path-graduation)

### Inferences
- The UBC wheel is the most important single data point for this redesign: a single completion percentage is the most glanceable element and also the first to be wrong, and when it is wrong it produces anxiety, not a shrug. A headline figure needs to be either provably the institution's own number or labelled as an estimate.
- Category bars (one bar per kind of requirement) are the one chart every vendor converges on; pie charts of hours appear and are then switched off for complex programs.
- "In progress counted as not satisfied" (Workday) versus "in progress counted toward the bar" (Degree Works option, uAchieve blue) is a real fork; the tools that show it as a third state get fewer "is this broken" questions than the one that folds it into "unsatisfied".

### Gaps
- EAB Navigate, EduNav (SmartPlan), PeopleSoft's Academic Advisement Report and Coursedog's own audit UI: no presentation details were found in search; nothing is claimed about them here.
- The low-authority claim that Modo Labs / Kuali offer a "mobile-first degree audit" comes from a listicle and is unverified. — [Gitnux listicle](https://gitnux.org/best/degree-audit-software/)

## 4. Penn: Path@Penn's audit and worksheet, Path Plans, and Penn Labs' Penn Degree Plan

### Takeaway
Penn's official tools are an audit with per-section percentage bars that shows only declared programs, a separate "Explore Programs" what-if, and a "Path Plans" schedule validator the College itself says is not a degree-planning tool; the student-built alternative, Penn Degree Plan, is a semester grid plus a requirements panel plus a course dock, and its November 2026 redesign moved to one tab and one progress ring per program after its own progress maths was found to over-count.

### Cited Findings
- The audit is reached from "Degree planning and audit" under Academic Planning and Registration; the College says it "accurately shows your progress to degree" and has percentage bars for each part of an Arts and Sciences degree. — [Penn College: Interpreting Your Degree Audit](https://www.college.upenn.edu/interpreting-degree-audit); [Penn SRFS: Perform a Degree Audit in PATH](https://apps.srfs.upenn.edu/secure/Pennant-Training/Students/Degree-Audit/Perform-a-Degree-Audit-in-PATH.pdf)
- "Path's Degree Audit section shows only worksheets for your currently-declared majors. However, under the Explore Programs section you can view worksheets for other majors/degrees." Courses "can be dragged and dropped across degree requirements" on the worksheet (CIS advising). — [Penn CIS advising: worksheet](https://advising.cis.upenn.edu/worksheet/)
- The CIS advising page keeps a list of "a variety of small issues with the current versions of the degree worksheets in Path" (page last updated September 2022 per the search extract, so possibly stale). — [Penn CIS advising: worksheet](https://advising.cis.upenn.edu/worksheet/)
- Penn Engineering treats the worksheet as a planning document made with the advisor: the first planning worksheet "is prepared during the first semester of the freshman year and modified as interests change"; the handbook has a FAQ titled "Which worksheet should I use?". — [Penn Engineering handbook FAQ](https://academics.engineering.upenn.edu/ugrad/student-handbook/faq/which-worksheet-should-i-use/)
- Path Plans (February 2025): lets students "create potential class schedules and validate them against their primary degree requirements" and "create snapshots of degree plans"; the College's site calls it "a course planning tool, not an academic or degree planning tool". A student-government member said it could work "in addition to the advising process". — [The Daily Pennsylvanian, 2025-02](https://www.thedp.com/article/2025/02/path-at-penn-releases-new-application); [Penn SRFS: PATH Creating Plans](https://apps.srfs.upenn.edu/secure/Pennant-Training/Students/PATH-Planner/PATH-Creating-Plans.pdf)
- A November 2025 DP column argues Path@Penn needs a chatbot that combines a student's record with questions such as how many credits remain and which courses fill a Sector requirement, because course selection is arduous enough that students take unwanted or duplicate classes or slow their degree progress. Opinion, one author. — [The Daily Pennsylvanian, 2025-11](https://www.thedp.com/article/2025/11/ai-chatbot-in-path-at-penn-course-registration)
- Penn Degree Plan (PDP) is one of four Penn Labs products on one shared codebase and API (Course Review, Course Plan, Course Alert, Degree Plan). — [pennlabs/penn-courses README](https://github.com/pennlabs/penn-courses)
- PDP's layout, from a third-party prototype that copies it: "periwinkle UI, semester planner, PCR stats, requirements panel, and course dock". — [jiyeonjungart/penn-degree-plan-prototype](https://github.com/jiyeonjungart/penn-degree-plan-prototype)
- PDP redesign, PR #821 "PDP Redesign - Requirements" (merged 2026-11-04): plans with more than one program get a tab per degree, major and minor; each tab shows a progress ring averaging that program's top-level rules; full program names with two or fewer programs, scrolling codes beyond that; last tab remembered per plan; a single-program plan keeps a header with a progress fill; each requirement's title, check mark and progress bar sit on one line above its courses; the whole requirement block accepts dropped courses; sticky rule headers coloured by depth with a program-coloured border. — [pennlabs/penn-courses PR #821](https://github.com/pennlabs/penn-courses/pull/821)
- The same PR fixes over-counted progress: a "pick N" rule used to sum all children, so satisfying extra options pushed it past 100% and removed its satisfied check, and "a parent section could then read 100% while a requirement was still missing"; the fix counts only the N furthest-along children, caps leaves at 100%, and shows 0% in place of NaN% for an empty section. A follow-up "PDP Redesign - Catch All #823" was linked two days later. — [pennlabs/penn-courses PR #821](https://github.com/pennlabs/penn-courses/pull/821)
- Penn Labs' own design write-up for Penn Course Plan notes that each Penn school has its own graduation requirements. — [Penn Labs blog: The Design of Penn Course Plan](https://pennlabs.org/blog/the-design-of-penn-course-plan-under-the-hood/)
- Other student-built Penn planners exist (PennPlanner: "drag and drop courses to plan your entire degree journey"; a 4-year CS planner; penndegreeplanner.com), which shows unmet demand for a semester-grid planner. — [qu8n/pennplanner](https://github.com/qu8n/pennplanner); [shivani9298/PennClassPlanner](https://github.com/shivani9298/PennClassPlanner); [penndegreeplanner.com](https://penndegreeplanner.com/)

### Inferences
- Penn Labs, working for the same students, arrived at "one tab and one ring per program" for multi-program students three weeks before this research. That is the closest prior art to the app's "both" problem, and it chose separate rings over a combined figure.
- PDP's over-count bug is the same class of error this app has already met (a ring that reads whole while a requirement is open, or the reverse). Any ring computed by the app rather than read from Penn needs the "pick N" rule and a cap.
- Penn's official stack splits the job across three surfaces (audit, Explore Programs, Path Plans) with a disclaimer on the third. The opportunity is one surface; the risk is that students already distrust a planner that "is not a degree planning tool".
- The DP column's two example questions ("how many credits remain", "which courses fill a Sector") are "what's left" and "does this count" again.

### Gaps
- No DP article on Penn Degree Plan's launch or reception was found; a 2023 DP piece listing Penn Labs' products does not include it, so it launched later. Its reception among students is unknown.
- The College's "Interpreting Your Degree Audit" page and the CIS issues list could not be read in full (hosts blocked), so the specific symbols, the handling of double counts and petitions in Path's audit, and the itemised issues are not in these notes. The repo's own capture of Path@Penn (`docs/DEGREE_PROGRESS_V12_BUILD_SPEC.md`) is the better source for the audit's actual structure.
- PR #821 has no screenshots; the layout description is from the PR text.

## 5. Student-built and third-party planners: what students praise

### Takeaway
The student-built tools that earn loyalty are semester grids you can rearrange freely, with requirements checked live at the side and many saved alternative plans; praise centres on speed, freedom to experiment, and seeing the whole four years at once, not on audit fidelity.

### Cited Findings
- CourseRoad (MIT, SIPB, open source): a four-year planner that shows each major's requirements, when classes run and average weekly hours from evaluations; an MIT first-year guide calls it "probably the best scheduling resource". It won MIT's iCampus Student Prize first round in 2012. — [sipb/courseroad2](https://github.com/sipb/courseroad2); [MIT first-year guide](https://firstyear.mit.edu/?p=625); [iCampus Prize 2012](https://icampusprize.mit.edu/2012/03/courseroad-2012-first-round-winner)
- Student testimony: one MIT blogger spent many hours on CourseRoad as a freshman and saved at least 25 alternative plans; a 2012 post calls it an indispensable part of campus life. Admissions-blog posts by students, so anecdotal. — [MIT Admissions blog (p=55679)](https://mitadmissions.org/?p=55679); [MIT Admissions blog (p=6491)](https://mitadmissions.org/?p=6491)
- CourseRoad does not guarantee a plan satisfies requirements: a student built a deliberately unrealistic road that broke a General Institute Requirement rule. — [MIT Admissions blog (p=94457)](https://mitadmissions.org/?p=94457)
- MonPlan (Monash, student-built, later adopted): testimonials on its own site stress time saved, a clean interface, and being "easier to use than Excel". Promotional. — [The MonPlan Project](https://monplan.github.io/)
- CourseFix (ACM preliminary study of a registration and degree-planning platform): students praised a clean view of degree and semester progress, sample-schedule integration, prerequisite/co-requisite checking and freedom to change selections; they asked for drag-and-drop four-year plans, course reviews and ratings, and mobile push reminders. The authors conclude current systems fail "not because they lack features, but because they misalign with how students naturally organize their planning processes". — [CourseFix, ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3764687.3769937)
- uCredit (Johns Hopkins student project, "Improving advising for undergraduate degree plans") is a published case study of a student-built planner. — [uCredit case study](https://jamnavarro.github.io/ucredit)
- CPAS (Cornell, ASEE 2009): described as the first degree-audit system built from the start with a web graphical interface for students and advisors to track progress. — [ASEE PEER: CPAS](https://peer.asee.org/cpas-on-the-structure-and-usability-of-a-course-planning-and-audit-system)
- Department-published maps and curriculum visualisations: UCSD's Curricular Analytics graph view highlights "high-DFW prerequisites and infrequently offered courses" in a major plan; an ASEE work-in-progress uses a metro-map metaphor for interrelated curricula; MyCourseMap is "an interactive visual map to increase curriculum transparency". None reports student comprehension results in the extracts found. — [UCSD curriculum graph view](https://educationalinnovation.ucsd.edu/curricular-analytics/views/graph.html); [ASEE metro-map paper](https://peer.asee.org/work-in-progress-a-metro-map-based-curriculum-visualization-for-examining-interrelated-curricula.pdf); [MyCourseMap](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396416475_MyCourseMap_An_interactive_visual_map_to_increase_curriculum_transparency_for_university_students_and_staff)

### Inferences
- Saving 25 alternative plans is a strong signal: students use these tools to think, not to file a plan. Cheap duplication and comparison of plans matters more than one perfect plan.
- Every well-liked student tool is a grid of terms first and a requirements checker second. The official tools invert that (rule list first, plan second), and are the ones described as hard to read.
- The CourseFix conclusion (misalignment with how students organise planning, not missing features) supports cutting features over adding them.

### Gaps
- Nothing usable was found on Coursicle, Atlas (Michigan), Carta (Stanford), UW Flow, Berkeleytime or Hydrant/Firehose in this session; they are mainly course-discovery and schedule tools, and no claims about them are made here.
- The uCredit case study page could not be fetched, so its user-research findings are not included.
- No study comparing prerequisite flowcharts with semester grids for student comprehension was found.

## 6. Research: usability and learning-analytics studies, and what students want to know

### Takeaway
The research base specific to degree audits is thin and small-sample, but consistent: students grasp "complete vs not complete" quickly, struggle with adding planned courses and with anything shown without context, lose trust when the tool does not match a non-standard path (transfer, AP, exceptions), and national survey data says only about half of undergraduates have been helped to review degree progress at all.

### Cited Findings
- Texas Law UX case study ("Building a Better Degree Audit"): the original audit "didn't list all the degree requirements needed to graduate". In quick prototype tests, most students understood generating an audit with filters; on adding planned courses, half fully understood and half "sort of" understood; the team reframed "generate an audit" as a filtering panel. Informal, small sample. — [Texas Law UX case study](https://law.utexas.edu/ux-case-studies/degree-audit/)
- Penn State case study (INTED 2014, "Designing student information systems that work for students"): a follow-up survey asked whether students use the audit, use it correctly and find it usable; about half of freshmen would use the audit to pick next-semester courses; conclusion: usability must be a priority. Abstract only. — [IATED: Wijekumar 2014](https://library.iated.org/view/WIJEKUMAR2014DES)
- Klein et al. 2019 (81 undergraduates in focus groups, a large public university): students said dashboard data often did not match their degree requirements and missed outside credit such as AP or honors; the degree-progress tool worked for students on standard plans (for one such student it was "useful, reliable, and can spur her to action") and struggled with valid unconventional paths; information without context left students unable to relate it to their progress; the authors tie sensemaking to relevance, accuracy and context and say visualisations "without context and clarity" affect trust. — [Klein et al., J. Educational Technology Systems](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0047239519859854)
- A secondary review of the same line of work reports some students felt "turned off" by a dashboard and its predictive data, and ignored it or acted against it. — [Journal of Learning Analytics systematic review](https://learning-analytics.info/index.php/JLA/article/view/8093)
- CHI 2019 ("It's My Data!"): students worried that advisors seeing detailed performance data could bias how they were advised. — [ACM CHI 2019](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3290605.3300824)
- LAK sensemaking study: recommends pairing visualisations with tailored messages and training, because charts alone are not interpreted reliably. — [ACM: students' sensemaking of LADs](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3303772.3303804)
- Notre Dame dashboard study (CSEDU 2022): participants could read the charts as designed, yet an instructor worried a rank display could discourage students, since a low rank is not poor performance. — [CSEDU 2022 paper](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~cwang11/papers/csedu22-lad.pdf)
- A 2025 paper on explainable dashboards argues that when students cannot tell how data was analysed or why a recommendation appears, buy-in and comprehension suffer. — [arXiv 2506.16312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.16312)
- Inside Higher Ed / College Pulse Student Voice survey (2023, 3,004 undergraduates): 55% say they received guidance on required courses and sequences needed to graduate; 52% say they got help reviewing degree progress to make sure they are on track; with required advising meetings those rise to 69% and 64%. — [Inside Higher Ed, 2023-02](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/academic-life/2023/02/28/student-survey-reveals-gaps-core-academic-advising)
- NACADA Journal survey (Hong Kong, 2017): students ranked understanding study options and graduation requirements as the most important aspect of advising, planning studies second; they wanted "a two-way interactive relationship" and options; their greatest stated need was discussing careers. — [NACADA Journal 37(2)](https://meridian.allenpress.com/nacada-journal/article/37/2/21/36401/Survey-of-Needs-and-Expectations-for-Academic)
- NACADA national assessment study: the advising outcome most often measured is "student knows the degree requirements of college/department", and listed learning outcomes include being able to interpret a degree audit report. Reading an audit is treated as a skill to be taught. — [NACADA Journal 34(1)](https://nacada.kglmeridian.com/view/journals/jnaa/34/1/article-p64.xml)
- AACRAO/Coursedog (653 respondents): students and advisors trust audit accuracy less than the staff who run the audits. — [Coursedog / AACRAO white paper](https://coursedog.com/whitepaper/aacrao-use-of-degree-audit-and-education-planning-solutions)

### Inferences
- The three questions in the brief ("when do I graduate", "what do I take next", "does this count") are supported indirectly: surveys measure "required courses and sequences" and "am I on track", the Stellic ticket name is literally "why isn't this counting", and the Penn State finding is that students open the audit to pick next semester's courses. No study found asks students to rank those questions directly.
- The Klein finding predicts where this app will be judged: dual-degree students, transfer/AP credit, petitions and what-ifs are all "unconventional paths", exactly where degree-progress tools lose trust.
- The profession treats "can interpret a degree audit" as a learning outcome. A presentation that needs no teaching would be unusual, and is the bar the owner is asking for.
- Planned courses are the comprehension weak spot (Texas Law), not completed ones. The riskiest part of the design is the boundary between "done", "registered" and "I intend to".

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed controlled usability study of a degree audit's layout was found; the evidence is one practitioner case study, one abstract, one thesis and adjacent dashboard research.
- No study was found of students' understanding of what-if audits, of double counting displays, or of AI-generated degree plans.
- The Texas State thesis's survey numbers and the Texas Law page's verbatim student quotes were not retrievable.

## 7. How these tools show double counting, exceptions, in-progress vs completed, dual degrees, and what-if

### Takeaway
Double counting is mostly invisible (applied by an engine, revealed by a hover icon at best); exceptions live in advisor workflows and surface to students only as a requirement that silently turns complete; in-progress has a settled three-state convention; and dual degrees are the weakest area of every tool, handled by toggles, tabs or separate what-if runs, never by a combined figure.

### Cited Findings
- Double counting, Degree Works: built to let one course satisfy more than one requirement where rules allow; CUNY City Tech calls it "double-duty" and states which pairs may share (major with general-education Pathways or College Option). The course simply appears under both requirements. — [CUNY City Tech Degree Works](https://www.citytech.cuny.edu/registrar/degreeworks.aspx)
- Double counting, Stellic: the audit applies the institution's double-counting rules to each requirement "in an order determined by how the audit is configured"; a course that could satisfy both a major and a gen-ed requirement may be allowed once, or be placed "where it's most efficient for the student's overall progress". On screen: a right-side icon "Course is counting toward multiple requirements, hover for details" (UWM); "the course is double counting with a requirement in another program" (Case Western); a tooltip above a double-check icon (UNK). — [Stellic: how an audit decides where a course counts](https://www.stellic.com/resources/how-degree-audit-decides-where-courses-count); [UWM student guide](https://uwm.edu/registrar/wp-content/uploads/sites/671/2026/03/Audit-Icons-3.2.26.pdf); [Case Western audit icons](https://case.edu/registrar/sites/default/files/2024-02/Stellic%20-%20Audit%20Icons.pdf); [UNK: reviewing student progress](https://www.unk.edu/offices/registrar/stellic/stellic-advisor-resources1/degree-audit.php)
- Stellic's student-facing explainer on double majors: almost every institution caps shared coursework "somewhere, and it's rarely a single ceiling"; gen-ed and cognate courses are the most likely to count twice and a major's core the least; unfilled requirement slots are the visible sign of a gap; run a what-if before declaring. Vendor content. — [Stellic: can you double major](https://www.stellic.com/resources/can-you-double-major)
- "Taken but cannot count here" has its own state in Stellic: Western Seminary's support article lists causes (failed, withdrawn, incomplete, P/F where not allowed, already used for another requirement), and UVA tells students to check the course "has not been used to fulfill another requirement in the audit". — [Western Seminary support](https://support.westernseminary.edu/support/solutions/articles/63000273257-a-course-is-not-counting-towards-my-degree-in-stellic-what-should-i-do-); [UVA Stellic student features](https://stellic.virginia.edu/student-features)
- Exceptions, Stellic: any advisor may request an exception in the tool; it routes to an approver before it changes the audit; types are substitution (most common), waive or modify a constraint, exclude courses, move excess credits; "a justification is required for every exception request". Columbia warns that incorrect or excessive exceptions misalign the record with graduation requirements. — [Trinity College adviser training](https://www.trincoll.edu/registrar/wp-content/uploads/sites/120/2025/09/Trinity-College_Stellic-Adviser-User-Training.pdf); [Columbia SIS: program exceptions and waivers](https://sishelp.sis.columbia.edu/content/program-exceptions-and-waivers); [UD advising: common advisee questions](https://www.advising.udel.edu/for-advisors/advisor-training-professional-development/answers-to-common-advisee-questions/)
- Exceptions, Degree Works: the route is a petition, prompted by a course sitting in Fallthrough; an audit "missing an approved petition" is a named reason to contact the degree office (UAA). Texas State's graduate college publishes a guide to submitting petitions through Degree Works. — [Charlotte FAQ](https://ninercentral.charlotte.edu/?p=2627); [UAA help](https://www.uaa.alaska.edu/students/progress/degreeworks-help.cshtml); [Texas State: submitting petitions](https://www.gradcollege.txst.edu/faculty-staff/guide/submitting-petitions-guide.html)
- Exceptions and what-if interact badly: Stellic's API documentation says exceptions that do not fit the rebuilt requirements can be marked removed, and advises against what-ifs for students with exceptions. — [Stellic partner API](https://docs.stellic.com/stellic-partner-api/student/run-a-what-if-audit-for-a-student)
- In-progress vs completed: three states everywhere. Degree Works: Complete / In-Progress / Incomplete block labels, blue or "REG" for in-progress, and a toggle to include in-progress and preregistered courses. uAchieve at Miami: green / blue / orange. Stellic: green check / orange clock / planned check / red box / grey dash for optional. Workday: in-progress does not satisfy. — [Xavier info sheet](https://www.xavier.edu/registrar/documents/xavier-degreeworks-update-infosheet.pdf); [Miami University](https://miamioh.edu/onestop/academic-records/degree-audit-report.html); [UWM audit icons](https://uwm.edu/registrar/enrollment/stellic/audit-icons/); [Palm Beach State KB](https://help.palmbeachstate.edu/TDClient/1971/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=102717)
- Dual degrees / double majors, Degree Works: Clemson: "Due to limitations in Degree Works, Double Major and Dual Degree students cannot accurately view their Academic Audits on the default screen... students must run a What-if analysis for each individual major or degree." Lafayette: a BS+BS shows both programs in one audit, an AB+BS gets a caret to toggle between degrees. Syracuse: majors under different degree types appear in a drop-down. — [Clemson degree audit procedures](https://www.clemson.edu/registrar/registration-degreeworks/degree-works-guide.html); [Lafayette quick guide](https://registrar.lafayette.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/193/2021/04/DegreeWorks-Quick-Guide-All-Users.pdf); [Syracuse student view](https://answers.atlassian.syr.edu/wiki/spaces/degreeworks/pages/155910815)
- Dual degrees, Stellic and Penn Labs: UVA's audit "shows degree requirements for each major and minor as well as general education requirements" as separate lists; Penn Degree Plan's redesign gives each degree, major and minor its own tab and ring. — [UVA Stellic student features](https://stellic.virginia.edu/student-features); [pennlabs/penn-courses PR #821](https://github.com/pennlabs/penn-courses/pull/821)
- What-if comparison: no tool found shows current and hypothetical side by side. Degree Works re-renders the whole audit for the hypothetical and (at most schools) does not save it; Stellic recalculates for a chosen set of programs; UD describes what-ifs as showing "exactly how switching a major, adding a minor, or changing catalog years would affect their degree progress". — [FGCU](https://www.fgcu.edu/recordsandregistration/degree-works); [Stellic partner API](https://docs.stellic.com/stellic-partner-api/student/run-a-what-if-audit-for-a-student); [UD advising](https://www.advising.udel.edu/for-advisors/advisor-training-professional-development/answers-to-common-advisee-questions/)

### Inferences
- No surveyed tool shows a combined percentage across two degrees. The convention is one figure per program, switched by tab, toggle or drop-down. (This matches the app's existing rule against a combined percent.)
- The best available double-count treatment is a small mark on the course with the other requirement named on demand. Nobody shows the cap ("3 of 4 shared courses used"), although caps are the rule that surprises students.
- What-if is universally a full replacement view, not a comparison. A plain "what changes" summary (courses that newly count, requirements added, terms added) appears to be an open space; no evidence was found that any tool does it.
- Exceptions are an advisor-side workflow in every tool. Students see results, not pending requests, which is the "hallway approval" gap Stellic itself names.

### Gaps
- No screenshot-level description of how uAchieve, Workday or Path@Penn mark a shared course was found.
- No source describes how any tool shows a *pending* petition to the student.
- Whether any tool visualises the difference between a what-if and the current audit could not be confirmed; absence here means "not found", not "does not exist".

## 8. Mobile: does any of these have a good phone experience, and what do they cut?

### Takeaway
No evidence was found of a degree audit or planner with a genuinely good phone experience: vendors claim "responsive" or "mobile-friendly", schools publish how-to videos for reaching the audit on a phone, and no independent review, study or student quote about phone use of these tools turned up.

### Cited Findings
- Degree Works' current interface is named the "Responsive Dashboard"; Missouri State describes the audit as modern and mobile-friendly; Messiah publishes a separate how-to video for mobile access through the student portal. — [Missouri State: Degree Works](https://www.missouristate.edu/Registrar/FacultyAndStaff/degreeworks.htm); [Messiah: Degree Works audit](https://www.messiah.edu/undergraduate-registrar/degree-works-audit/)
- Stellic's product page stresses real-time progress and what-if scenarios and does not mention a mobile app or phone layout. — [Stellic Progress](https://www.stellic.com/progress)
- Stellic's and uAchieve's richest details depend on hover: "hover for details" on the multiple-requirements icon; tooltips on audit icons. — [UWM student guide](https://uwm.edu/registrar/wp-content/uploads/sites/671/2026/03/Audit-Icons-3.2.26.pdf); [UNK: reviewing student progress](https://www.unk.edu/offices/registrar/stellic/stellic-advisor-resources1/degree-audit.php)
- Planners in every tool surveyed are drag-and-drop grids (Stellic at CMU, Path's worksheet, Penn Degree Plan, CourseRoad). — [CMU Stellic page](https://www.cmu.edu/es/stellic/index.html); [Penn CIS advising: worksheet](https://advising.cis.upenn.edu/worksheet/); [pennlabs/penn-courses PR #821](https://github.com/pennlabs/penn-courses/pull/821)
- Students in the CourseFix study asked for mobile push reminders, i.e. the phone as the place for nudges, alongside a desktop-style four-year grid. — [CourseFix, ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3764687.3769937)
- A listicle claims Modo Labs / Kuali offer a "mobile-first degree audit and academic planning module"; low-authority and unverified. — [Gitnux listicle](https://gitnux.org/best/degree-audit-software/)

### Inferences
- These tools are designed for a wide screen and an advisor's desk; "responsive" means the long worksheet reflows, not that the task was rethought. Two of their core interactions (hover for detail, drag across a multi-column grid) do not exist or are awkward on a phone.
- A phone-first degree view therefore has no strong precedent to copy. That is a risk (no proven pattern) and an opening (nobody has done the cut).
- What a phone version would have to cut or replace, judging by what the desktop tools rely on: hover tooltips (replace with tap-to-expand words), the eight-column term grid (one term at a time, or a vertical list of terms), side-by-side requirements panel and plan (one at a time), and the full leftover-courses bins.

### Gaps
- No App Store reviews, student quotes or studies about using Degree Works, Stellic, uAchieve, Workday Student or Penn Degree Plan on a phone were found.
- Whether Penn Degree Plan or CourseRoad have a usable phone layout was not checked (sites not fetchable in this session).

## What to keep, what to avoid (synthesis across the eight questions)

### Takeaway
Keep the conventions students already know (three states, one figure per program, a term grid, "what this course would fill") and say in plain words why something does not count; avoid a single headline number the app cannot stand behind, leftover bins in system language, silent reshuffling, hover-only detail, and a combined dual-degree percentage.

### Cited Findings
Keep:
- Three states with consistent marks: done, in progress/registered, still needed, with "planned" as a visibly different fourth. Every tool converges here. — [UWM audit icons](https://uwm.edu/registrar/enrollment/stellic/audit-icons/); [Miami University](https://miamioh.edu/onestop/academic-records/degree-audit-report.html); [Xavier info sheet](https://www.xavier.edu/registrar/documents/xavier-degreeworks-update-infosheet.pdf)
- Official vs planned as two readings of the same list, not two screens. — [Guilford College: Using Stellic](https://sites.google.com/guilford.edu/guilfordcollege-usingstellic/home)
- One figure and one tab per program for multi-program students. — [pennlabs/penn-courses PR #821](https://github.com/pennlabs/penn-courses/pull/821); [Lafayette quick guide](https://registrar.lafayette.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/193/2021/04/DegreeWorks-Quick-Guide-All-Users.pdf)
- A "still needed only" condensed view. — [Texas Tech audit guide](https://www.depts.ttu.edu/degreeworks/Training/DWAuditGuide.pdf)
- "What would this course fill" shown at the moment of adding it, and suggestions that close the most open requirements. — [JHU Stellic student resources](https://info.stellic.jhu.edu/student-resources/)
- A semester grid with free rearrangement and cheap alternative plans. — [MIT Admissions blog (p=55679)](https://mitadmissions.org/?p=55679); [CourseFix, ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3764687.3769937)
- A mark on a course that counts in two places, with the other place named. — [UWM student guide](https://uwm.edu/registrar/wp-content/uploads/sites/671/2026/03/Audit-Icons-3.2.26.pdf)
- Recommended sequences ("pathways") as a starting plan to edit. — [Columbia Engineering: Stellic](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/academics/academic-resources/stellic)
- Context beside every figure (a sentence, not only a chart). — [Klein et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0047239519859854); [ACM: sensemaking of LADs](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3303772.3303804)

Avoid:
- A single progress wheel the tool cannot guarantee: UBC removed Workday's because it was inaccurate, and inaccuracy was "a major source of student anxiety". — [The Ubyssey](https://ubyssey.ca/news/workday-academic-progress-wheel/)
- Two headline percentages that disagree without explanation. — [Farmingdale student guide](https://www.farmingdale.edu/registrar/degree-works/pdf/fsc-degreeworks-student-manual.pdf)
- Progress maths that sums "pick N" options or exceeds 100%. — [pennlabs/penn-courses PR #821](https://github.com/pennlabs/penn-courses/pull/821)
- Leftover bins named in system terms (Fallthrough, Not Counted, Unmatched) with "see your advisor" as the only explanation. — [Appalachian State FAQ](https://faa.appstate.edu/node?page=8); [FSU Stellic FAQ](https://stellic.fsu.edu/students/stellic-faq)
- Courses that move between requirements without notice ("best fit"). — [UGA Degree Works FAQ](https://reg.uga.edu/general-information/faqs/degreeworks.html)
- What-ifs that vanish, cannot be shared, or quietly drop exceptions. — [Wake Forest FAQ](https://registrar.wfu.edu/?p=923); [Stellic partner API](https://docs.stellic.com/stellic-partner-api/student/run-a-what-if-audit-for-a-student)
- Folding in-progress into "not satisfied". — [Palm Beach State KB](https://help.palmbeachstate.edu/TDClient/1971/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=102717)
- A default view that is wrong for dual-degree students. — [Clemson degree audit procedures](https://www.clemson.edu/registrar/registration-degreeworks/degree-works-guide.html)
- Detail that exists only on hover. — [UWM student guide](https://uwm.edu/registrar/wp-content/uploads/sites/671/2026/03/Audit-Icons-3.2.26.pdf)
- Moving familiar things for existing users without a bridge: upperclassmen at IU preferred the old tool and sought more help. — [Indiana Daily Student, 2026-10](https://www.idsnews.com/article/2026/10/iu-stellic-class-scheduling-advising-how-to)

### Inferences
- The recurring student question is not "what percent am I" but "why isn't this counting" and "what is left". A design that leads with what is left and explains each non-count in one line addresses the documented pain more directly than a better ring.
- No tool found leads with a graduation term, although "am I on track to graduate" is what surveys measure. That is absent from the prior art, so there is no evidence either way on how students read it.
- Because every source shows trust breaking on non-standard cases, the presentation of uncertainty (planned, pending approval, estimated by the app vs stated by Penn) is the part with the least prior art and the most risk.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found on how students respond to an AI-filled plan in any of these tools, or to advisor questions surfaced by the tool.
- Nothing here measures comprehension of any of the "keep" patterns on a phone; they are conventions from desktop tools.
